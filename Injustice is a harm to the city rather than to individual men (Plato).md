What distinction do the Laws make between the injustice of Socrates' conviction and the injustice of his potential escape?
#### Notes
**Nature of Proposition**: Conceptual Distinction / Core Logical Pillar. **Philosophical Concept**: Procedural Justice vs. Substantive Injustice. **Inferential Logic**: Socrates' death sentence was an injustice committed against him by _men_ (the jury/Athenian citizens), not by the _Laws_ themselves, which are structurally just. If Socrates escapes, he retaliates not against those individual men, but against the Laws and the city themselves, attempting to destroy them. Therefore, escaping is a moral category error: it attempts to cure an injustice by committing a direct, active injustice against an innocent and superior entity (the Laws). **Citation**: _Crito_, 54b–c [Passage 10].
