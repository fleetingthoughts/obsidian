What are the logical requirements for constructing the "perfectly unjust man" and perfectly "just man" in Glaucon's comparison of lives?
#### Notes
He must act like a master craftsman (such as a doctor or pilot) who knows his art's limits (Book 2, 360e–361a). He must execute the greatest injustices while securing the most perfect reputation for justice, and have the strength, courage, and resources to correct any mistakes if his deeds come to light (Book 2, 361a–b).

The just man must be a simple and noble person who does not wish to _seem_ good, but to _be_ good (Book 2, 361b). To ensure he is not practicing justice for the sake of honors or rewards, he must be stripped of everything except justice, going unchanged till death with the greatest reputation for injustice (Book 2, 361c–d).
