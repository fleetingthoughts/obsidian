Why is the pleasure of non-philosophers argued to be a mere "shadow painting" (_skiagraphia_) and not true pleasure?
#### Notes
**Argued Position.**<br>• _Premise_: "Repose" is the neutral, intermediate state of the soul between pleasure and pain (Book 9, 583c).<br>• _Argument_: When a sick man experiences the cessation of pain, he praises this absence of suffering as the highest pleasure, though it is merely neutral repose. Similarly, when a man's physical enjoyment ceases, the repose feels painful (583c–e). Because these physical experiences are merely shifts in state (motions of the soul), it is argued that non-philosophers mistake the relief from pain for pure pleasure, and the relief from pleasure for pure pain. This contrast creates a cognitive illusion or "wizardry" that is not true, pure pleasure, but a mere shadow painting (Book 9, 583e–584a).
