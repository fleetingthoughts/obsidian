How does Socrates use the example of the three couchmakers to demonstrate that imitative art (_mimesis_) is ontologically "three steps removed from the truth"? (Book 10, 596a–597e)
#### Notes
**Argued Position.**<br>• _Premise_: According to the Theory of Forms, there is exactly one perfect, divine Form for each class of physical particulars (596a). This Form is produced by God as its "nature-begetter" to avoid an infinite regress, since if God made even two, a third would appear whose Form they both possess, and that third would be the real Form (597c–d).<br>• _Premise_: The human craftsman (the carpenter) builds a physical couch by looking toward the Form, producing not absolute Being (_what is_), but a physical particular that is merely _like_ Being (597a, 597d).<br>• _Argument_: Because the painter does not copy the Form, but merely represents the physical couch produced by the carpenter, his creation is a copy of a copy. Therefore, the imitator's product stands at the third generation from nature, making imitation three steps removed from the truth and pure Being (Book 10, 597d–e).
