Why does Hempel argue that overt physical behavior cannot be described in a purely non-psychological, behavioristic vocabulary without relying on psychological terms? What example does he use to illustrate this.
#### Notes
**Nature of Proposition: Logical Argument.** Whether a physical action qualifies as a specific behavioral disposition (e.g., courage or courtesy) depends inherently on the agent's internal cognitive states—specifically, **what the agent knows or believes** about the situation and **what objectives they want to attain**. Because we must refer to beliefs and desires to characterize these behavioral patterns, a purely physicalist, non-psychological vocabulary is logically insufficient to define psychological terms. (_Philosophy of Natural Science_, Chapter 8, pp. 108–109)

**Nature of Proposition: Illustrative Case Example.** A man walking unflinchingly toward a thicket containing a hungry lion is not acting courageously if he does not believe (or know) that the lion is there. Conversely, a man running away is not acting cowardly if he believes the thicket is on fire. Because the behavioral classification ("courageous") is logically dependent on the agent's internal beliefs and motives, we cannot define the psychological trait purely in terms of raw physical movements without presupposing other psychological concepts. (_Philosophy of Natural Science_, Chapter 8, p. 109)
