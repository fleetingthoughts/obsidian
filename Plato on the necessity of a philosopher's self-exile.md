How does Socrates justify the political withdrawal of the tiny remnant of true, uncorrupted philosophers?
#### Notes
**Argued Position.**<br>• _Premise_: A philosopher has no political ally with whom they could aid justice and be preserved, and trying to fight the public's lawlessness alone is like falling in with wild beasts (496c–d).<br>• _Argument_: Because a solitary just man would perish before being of use to himself, his friends, or the city, it is argued that the uncorrupted philosopher (such as those saved by exile, physical sickness like Theages, or the demonic sign) must stand aside under a wall during a storm of lawlessness, keeping quiet, minding their own business, and contenting themselves with living a pure life (Book 6, 496a–497a).
