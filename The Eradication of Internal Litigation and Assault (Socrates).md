Articulate how the abolition of private property and the establishment of generational kinship naturally eradicate lawsuits and physical assault from the guardian class. State how this answer's Glaucon's question in the beginning of book 4. Summarize how Socrates conclusively resolves Adeimantus's earlier objection regarding the guardians' happiness by comparing their lifestyle to that of Olympic victors.
#### Notes
Because guardians own nothing but their bodies, faction over money and property vanishes, eliminating the basis for lawsuits (464d–e). Assault against elders is prevented by two internalized "guardians": _shame_ (reverence for a perceived parent) and _fear_ (that other "sons" or "brothers" will come to the victim's aid) (465a–b).

Socrates argues the guardians live a life more blessed than Olympic victors. While victors win personal glory, the guardians' victory is the preservation of the entire city, for which they are crowned with lifelong public support, freedom from poverty, and an honorable burial (465d–466a).
