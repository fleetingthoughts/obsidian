What is Socrates' core moral principle regarding retaliation, and how does it contrast with conventional Greek morality and that Socrates specifically questions Crito as consideration for the rest of his arguments
#### Notes
**Nature of Proposition**: Core Deontological Constraint. **Philosophical Concept**: Deontological Anti-Retaliation (rejection of _Lex Talionis_). **Inferential Logic**: Socrates establishes that: (1) one must never do wrong willingly, as wrongdoing is always harmful and shameful to the wrongdoer; (2) therefore, one must never do wrong in return when wronged, nor mistreat anyone in return when mistreated. Socrates explicitly rejects the popular Greek view that justice consists of helping friends and harming enemies (retaliation). He holds that retaliation is always unjust because it involves doing harm, which is equivalent to wrongdoing. **Citation**: _Crito_, 49a–e [Passage 6, 7].
