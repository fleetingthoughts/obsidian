What logical error underlies the postulation of "real non-existent entities" (such as imaginary objects, universals, or abstract propositions) in metaphysics?
#### Notes
**Grammatical Superstition and Referential Fallacy**: Logical elimination of subsistence.<br><br>**The Error**: Metaphysicians assume that to every word or phrase that can be the grammatical subject of a sentence, there must be a corresponding real entity.<br><br>**The Metaphysical Solution**: Since objects like "unicorns" or abstract "universals" do not exist in the physical, empirical world, metaphysicians invoke a "special non-empirical world" (a Platonic realm of subsistence or abstract entities) to house them.<br><br>**Refutation (Unstated Assumption)**: This is based on a false referential theory of meaning. Logical analysis (specifically Bertrand Russell's Theory of Descriptions) shows that we can analyze sentences containing terms like "the round square" or "unicorns" without assuming those terms denote any existing or subsisting entity. These grammatical subject terms can be logically eliminated through quantification.<br><br>**Citation**: Chapter 1, Page 43.
