Why does Hempel reject N. R. Campbell's claim that a valuable scientific theory must display an analogy to known, familiar laws?
#### Notes
**Nature of Proposition: Epistemological Argument.** Campbell argues that theories must be modeled on familiar phenomena (like water waves or billiard balls) to be explanatory. Hempel argues that this confuses objective scientific understanding with subjective, psychological feelings of "at-homeness." The goal of scientific explanation is objective systematic unification under testable basic principles, not subjective comfort. If necessary, science will explain the familiar by reduction to highly unfamiliar, counter-intuitive principles, as occurred with relativity and quantum mechanics. (_Philosophy of Natural Science_, Chapter 6, pp. 82-84)
