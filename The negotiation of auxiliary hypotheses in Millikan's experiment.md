How did the historical dispute between Robert Millikan and Felix Ehrenhaft over the quantization of electric charge demonstrate how physicists negotiate the falsification of auxiliary assumptions?
#### Notes
**Nature of Proposition: Historical Case Example.** When Ehrenhaft reported finding subelectronic charges, Millikan defended his core hypothesis of quantized electronic charge by arguing that Ehrenhaft's experiments violated crucial test conditions (droplet evaporation, dust contamination, non-spherical shapes), which functioned as auxiliary assumptions. Interestingly, Millikan's own slight numerical error was later traced to a false auxiliary hypothesis regarding the exact viscosity of air. (_Philosophy of Natural Science_, Chapter 3, pp. 24-25)
