What is the psychological mechanism by which imitative poetry succeeds in corrupting even highly virtuous individuals? (Book 10, 605c–606d)
#### Notes
**Argued Position.**<br>• _Premise_: When we experience personal tragedy, we pride ourselves on holding down our grief, believing restraint to be the manly path (605d–e).<br>• _Argument_: When we watch a tragic hero lamenting on stage, we feel no shame in letting our guard down because it is another's suffering. We allow our emotional, crying part—which is normally held down by force—to feast on pity and tears, believing there is no harm (605d–606a). However, it is argued that only a rare few can calculate the **unconscious infection**: the pitying part, fed strong on theatrical examples, becomes so robust that it can no longer be held down when we face our own personal misfortunes, thereby destroying our psychological self-mastery (Book 10, 606b).
