The specific conclusion drawn from the "Ship of State" analogy regarding the philosopher's perceived uselessness in the city.
#### Notes
**Argued Position.**<br>• _Premise_: In a ship where the shipowner (the populace) is deaf, short-sighted, and ignorant of navigation, and the mutinous sailors (demagogues/politicians) fight for the rudder without having ever learned the art of piloting, they praise the cleverest manipulator as a "skilled sailor" and mock the true pilot who studies the stars, winds, and seasons as a useless "stargazer" (488a–489a).<br>• _Argument_: Because ruling is a distinct expertise (_techne_) requiring knowledge of cosmic and environmental conditions (the Forms), and because those who run cities do not understand or value this art, it is argued that the philosopher's uselessness is not the fault of the philosopher, but of the cities who fail to use them, just as it is not the doctor's place to beg the sick to be cured (Book 6, 488a–489c).
