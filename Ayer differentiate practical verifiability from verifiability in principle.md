Explain the logical distinction between "practical verifiability" and "verifiability in principle," using Ayer's classical examples.
#### Notes
**Methodological Distinction**: Operational vs. theoretical possibility.<br><br>**Practical Verifiability (Definition & Example)**: A statement is practically verifiable if we currently possess the technological and physical means to place ourselves in a position to observe its truth or falsehood (e.g., verifying that a key is in a drawer).<br><br>**Verifiability in Principle (Definition & Example)**: A statement is verifiable in principle if we currently lack the physical or practical means to verify it, but we can theoretically conceive of and formulate the exact observations that would decide its truth or falsehood if we were in the position to make them. Ayer's example is the proposition "there are mountains on the farther side of the moon" (verifiable in principle, as we know what observations would decide it, even if space flight was not yet practically achieved in 1936).<br><br>**Metaphysical Counter-Example**: In contrast, F. H. Bradley's metaphysical assertion that "the Absolute enters into, but is itself incapable of, evolution and progress" is not even verifiable in principle, because we cannot conceive of _any_ sense-experience that would confirm or deny it.<br><br>**Citation**: Chapter 1, Pages 35–36.
