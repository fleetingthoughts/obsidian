Why is the political tyrant actually far more wretched than the private man with tyrannic desires?
#### Notes
**Argued Position.**<br>• _Premise_: The private tyrannic man can at least hide his desires and live safely within a law-abiding city (Book 9, 579c).<br>• _Argument_: The political tyrant is like a sick man without bodily control who is forced to spend his life fighting and contesting with others. He is locked in a prison of his own making, unable to travel or see the sights free men enjoy; stuck in his house, he lives in envy, surrounded by enemies, forced to rule others while having no control over himself, making him the ultimate, friendless slave to the most worthless men (Book 9, 579c–e).
