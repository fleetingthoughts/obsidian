tWhat are the three components of the composite image of the soul that Socrates molds in speech, and how are they arranged? How does the composite image of the soul define the exact meaning of praising "injustice" versus praising "justice"?
#### Notes
**Premise.** Socrates molds a three-part composite image of the soul:<br>1. **The Multitudinous Beast**: A many-colored, many-headed monster with a ring of heads of tame and savage beasts that can grow and change them from itself (the desiring part) (588c);<br>2. **The Lion**: A spirited, strong, and victory-loving beast (the spirited part) (588d); and<br>3. **The Human Being**: A rational, smaller figure representing intellect (the calculating part) (588d).<br>These three are joined together into one natural growth, and covered on the outside by the shell of a human being so that it looks like a single animal to an outside observer (Book 9, 588b–e).

<br>• _Argument_: It is argued that:<br>(1) Praising **injustice** means asserting that it is profitable to feast and strengthen the many-headed beast and the lion while starving and weakening the inner human being, leaving him to be dragged wherever the beasts lead without making them friends (588e–589a); whereas<br>(2) Praising **justice** means asserting that the inner human being must be in absolute control, taking charge of the many-headed beast like a farmer—cultivating the tame heads, hindering the savage ones, making the lion an ally, and rearing all three parts as friends with each other and himself (Book 9, 589a–b).
