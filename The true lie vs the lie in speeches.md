What are the two classifications Socrates makes to distinguish between lies and his motivation for doing so
#### Notes
Socrates makes a sharp distinction between a falsehood in the soul and a falsehood expressed in language
- **The Lie in Words A spoken lie is merely an "imitation" or "phantom" (_eidōlon_) of the condition in the soul, coming into being after it, rather than being an unadulterated lie itself 
- **When a Spoken Lie is Useful (Not Deserving Hatred):** While a lie in the soul is always harmful and hated, Socrates argues that a **spoken lie can be useful to humans as a preventive drug or tool** in three specific situations 
    1. **Against Enemies:** To protect against hostility or harm.
    2. **As Preventive Medicine for Friends:** When friends out of folly or madness attempt to do something evil, a lie acts like a protective drug (_pharmakon_) to stop them.
    3. **In Mythology / Ancient Tales:** When humans lack certainty about ancient truths, they can "liken the lie to the truth as best can" to make the tale beneficial 

The motivation behind this distinction is due to the immutability of god and the common depiction of God's making use of these lies 

After establishing when spoken lies are useful for mortals, Socrates tests each of those three cases against the divine nature to see if God would ever lie 

- **No Lying Poet in God:** God has no ignorance of ancient things, so He never needs to construct false myths.
- **No Fear of Enemies:** God does not fear enemies, so He has no need to deceive them [118–119].
- **No Mad or Foolish Friends:** None of the foolish or mad are friends to the gods, so God never needs to use a spoken lie as preventive medicine on a friend.

**Conclusion:** Because none of the reasons that make spoken lies useful to humans apply to the divine, **God is entirely simple, true in deed and speech, and does not alter Himself or deceive humans through illusions, speeches, or dream signs**
