What is Socrates' critique of the non-philosophic crowd, and how does the "cattle" metaphor illustrate their existential failure?
#### Notes
**Argued Position.**<br>• _Premise_: Human beings who lack experience in prudence and virtue are shut out from the upper ontological region of true Being (Book 9, 586a).<br>• _Argument_: It is argued that these individuals spend their lives wandering between the lower region (pain) and the middle (repose), never looking up at what is truly above or being filled with what really is. Like cattle, they keep their heads bent toward the earth and the table, feeding, fattening, and copulating. Driven by an insatiable desire to fill a part of themselves that cannot contain real things with things that lack true being, they kick and butt each other with iron horns and hoofs, killing one another in frustration (Book 8, 586a–b).
