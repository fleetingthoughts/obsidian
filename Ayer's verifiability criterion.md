What is Alfred Ayer's initial formulation of the Criterion of Verifiability in Chapter 1, and what defines a "pseudo-proposition" under this rule?
#### Notes
**Semantic Definition and Rule of Meaning**: Logical partitioning of significance.<br><br>**Definition of Factual Significance**: A sentence is factually significant to a given person if, and only if, they know how to verify the proposition it purports to express—meaning they know what observations would lead them, under certain conditions, to accept the proposition as being true (confirmation) or reject it as being false (disconfirmation).<br><br>**Definition of Pseudo-Proposition**: If a putative proposition is neither a formal tautology (analytically true) nor such that its assumed truth or falsehood is consistent with some future experience, it fails to be literally significant and is classified as a pseudo-proposition. It may possess emotional significance but lacks literal semantic meaning or truth-value.<br><br>**Citation**: Chapter 1, Page 35.
