How does the Tyrant support his mercenary army, and what is the final, tragic irony of the people's "freedom"?
#### Notes
**Argued Position.**<br>• _Premise_: A massive bodyguard of foreign drones and freed slaves requires continuous, enormous financial support.<br>• _Argument_: When the tyrant's sacred funds and plundered riches run out, he turns to his father (the people) for support. If the people indignantly refuse to support their son's "troublesome drinking fellows" and bid him leave, the tyrant disarms his father and strikes him with physical force. It is argued that the people, in trying to flee the "smoke" of oligarchic or gentle enslavement, fall directly into the "fire" of the bitterest and harshest enslavement—serving their own former slaves (Book 8, 568d–569c).
