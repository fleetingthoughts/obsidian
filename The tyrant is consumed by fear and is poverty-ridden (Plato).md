Why is the tyrannic soul necessarily poverty-ridden, insatiable, and consumed by fear?
#### Notes
**Argued Position.**<br>• _Premise_: The soul is isomorphic to the tyrannic city, which is chronically poor, fearful, and full of lamentation (Book 9, 577e–578a).<br>• _Argument_: Because the city under a tyrant is economically hollowed out by its rulers, and because the individual tyrant's desires are infinite and structurally incapable of satisfaction, it is argued that his soul is always poverty-ridden and insatiable. Furthermore, because he is surrounded by hostile forces, his soul is necessarily filled with as much fear, sighing, and suffering as the wretched city he resembles (Book 9, 577e–578a).
