What does Socrates assert that the guardians must prioritize protecting above all other administrative commands. 
#### Notes
**Argued Position.**<br>• _Premise_: A sound, uncorrupted education produces good natures, which in turn produce still better offspring capable of maintaining the civic circle (Book 4, 424a).<br>• _Argument_: Because a rigorous rearing produces sensible, self-regulating men who can easily discover for themselves all minor laws and conventions (such as manners, procreation, and contracts), it is argued that the state does not need to legislate minor rules if the primary educational structure is strictly preserved (Book 4, 423e-424a).

Changes to education is a threat to national security:
• _Premise_: The foundational styles and modes of music are structurally bound to the greatest political laws of the state (Damon's principle) (Book 4, 424c).<br>• _Argument_: Because seemingly harmless musical innovations introduce lawlessness under the guise of "play" and gradually seep beneath the surface to corrupt customs, contracts, and laws, it is argued that the guardians must build their primary defensive guardhouse in music to prevent total constitutional subversion (Book 4, 424c-e).

Education is required for civil well-behaved city which the letter of the law cannot hope t o regulate
True gentlemen do not require a written code to behave decently in daily commercial affairs (Book 4, 425d-e).<br>• _Argument_: Because passing and constantly revising minor commercial regulations in a corrupt state is as futile and endless as a sick person trying new drugs without reforming their licentious lifestyle (acting as if they are cutting off the heads of a Hydra), it is argued that a true lawgiver should ignore these administrative details, as they either follow naturally from a good education or accomplish nothing in a bad regime (Book 4, 425e-427a).
