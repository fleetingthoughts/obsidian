Prove the rank of any matrix equals the maximum number of its linearly independent; that is, the rank of a matrix is the dimension of the subspace generated by its columns.
#### Notes
- Express $\text{rank}(A)$ as $\dim(R(L_A))$.
- Evaluate $R(L_A)$ as the span of the image of the standard basis $\beta = \{e_1, \dots, e_n\}$.
- Identify each $L_A(e_j)$ directly as the $j$-th column of $A$.
- Conclude that $\text{rank}(A)$ is the dimension of the subspace spanned by the columns of $A$.
