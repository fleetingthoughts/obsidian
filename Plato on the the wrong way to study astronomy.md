Why does Socrates reject observational, empirical astronomy as a useless study for the soul?
#### Notes
**Argued Position.**<br>• _Premise_: No physical, visible thing can ever be the object of true knowledge, as there is no knowledge of sensible things (Book 7, 529b–c).<br>• _Argument_: Because the stars are visible, physical bodies, they are prone to material deviation and fall far short of the true mathematical movements of the really fast and slow (Book 7, 529c–d). Therefore, it is argued that treating the visible heaven as the ultimate truth is ridiculous; instead, astronomy must be studied by using celestial movements as beautiful patterns to investigate mathematical problems, letting the visible things in heaven go (Book 7, 529d–530c).
