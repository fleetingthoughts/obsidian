How does Socrates refute Adeimantus's objection that the guardians are deprived of personal happiness (such as private lands, gold, silver, and luxury)?
#### Notes
**Argued Position.**<br>• _Premise_: The primary objective of the founding law is not to secure the exceptional, isolated happiness of any single class, but to maximize the harmony and happiness of the city as a whole (Book 4, 420b-c).<br>• _Argument_: Because attaching a vulgar form of private happiness to the guardians would turn them into self-seeking individuals rather than true protectors (analogous to painting a statue's eyes a color so beautiful that they no longer look like eyes), it is argued that the state must restrict their private luxuries to preserve their specialized civic function (Book 4, 420c-421b).
