What is Nelson Goodman's criterion for distinguishing laws from accidental generalizations using conditional statements?
#### Notes
**Nature of Proposition: Epistemological Argument.** A genuine law can, whereas an accidental generalization cannot, serve to support counterfactual conditionals (statements of the form "If A had been the case, then B would have been the case" where in fact A is not the case) and subjunctive conditionals ("If A should come to pass, then B would"). For example, the law that paraffin melts above 60 degrees Celsius supports the conditional "if this candle had been put in boiling water, it would have melted," but "all rocks in this box contain iron" cannot support "if this pebble had been put in the box, it would contain iron." (_Philosophy of Natural Science_, Chapter 5, p. 56)
