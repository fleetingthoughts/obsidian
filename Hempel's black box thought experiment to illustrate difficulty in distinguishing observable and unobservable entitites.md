How does the "black box" thought experiment fail to establish a sharp, telling distinction between observable macroscopic entities and unobservable microscopic entities?
#### Notes
**Nature of Proposition: Epistemological Argument.** One might argue that we can verify a macroscopic "black box" hypothesis by opening it to directly inspect its gears or vacuum tubes, whereas molecular mechanisms cannot be inspected. Hempel argues that "observability" is not precisely delimited: while a vacuum tube is seen, verifying it is a "properly working vacuum tube" requires complex instrumental tests presupposing theoretical laws. Furthermore, we accept fine wires seen only with glasses, then objects seen with magnifying glasses, microscopes, and eventually Geiger counters. This gradual transition from macro-objects to subatomic particles makes any sharp division of reality arbitrary. (_Philosophy of Natural Science_, Chapter 6, pp. 81-82)
