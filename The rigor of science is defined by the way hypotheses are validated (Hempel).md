How does Hempel define the rigor of science analogously to math. In what "wider sense" does Hempel allow scientific inquiry to be characterized as "inductive," and how does this redefine the role of "rules of induction"? 
#### Notes
**Nature of Proposition: Conceptual Definition.** Science is inductive in a wider sense because it accepts hypotheses on the basis of empirical data that afford only partial, non-conclusive confirmation (rather than deductive certainty). Under this view, "rules of induction" are not rules of discovery, but rather **canons of validation** that presuppose both the data and the hypothesis are already given, stating criteria to determine the strength of the data's support. (_Philosophy of Natural Science_, Chapter 2, pp. 17–18 )
