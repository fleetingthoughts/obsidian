Why does Hempel conclude that a "crucial test" is logically impossible in a strict deductive sense?
#### Notes
**Nature of Proposition: Epistemological Argument.** First, a negative outcome cannot definitively refute a rival hypothesis because test implications depend on auxiliary assumptions, allowing the refuted theory to be preserved by modifying those assumptions. Second, a positive outcome cannot definitively prove the other hypothesis because scientific hypotheses assert general laws over an infinite number of cases (like Galileo's law of free fall), which can never be conclusively established by a finite set of observational data. (_Philosophy of Natural Science_, Chapter 3, pp. 27-28)
