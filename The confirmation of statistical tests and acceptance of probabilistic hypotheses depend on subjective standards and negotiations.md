How does the theory of statistical tests and decisions negotiate the practical acceptance or rejection of probabilistic hypotheses?
#### Notes
**Nature of Proposition: Methodological Rule.** Because statistical evidence cannot logically prove or disprove a probabilistic hypothesis, accepting or rejecting it requires setting standards. These standards depend on the gravity of making two kinds of error: rejecting a true hypothesis (Type I error) and accepting a false one (Type II error). In practical contexts, like testing a vaccine's safety, these standards are determined by weighing the serious physical and social consequences of both errors. (_Philosophy of Natural Science_, Chapter 5, p. 65)
