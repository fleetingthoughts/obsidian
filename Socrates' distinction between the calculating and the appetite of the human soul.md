How does the experience of physical thirst demonstrate the separation between the Calculating Part (_Logistikon_) and the Desiring Part (_Epithymetikon_) of the soul?
#### Notes
**Argued Position.**<br>• _Premise_: Thirst as a natural desire is strictly a desire for drink itself, not for "good" or "bad" drink, and it impels the soul directly toward consumption (Book 4, 437b-438a, 439a-b).<br>• _Argument_: Because some thirsty individuals actively refuse to drink, and because the Principle of Non-Contradiction dictates that one part cannot simultaneously bid and forbid the same action, it is argued that the part bidding them to drink (desire) is separate from the part forbidding them (calculation), proving the soul has at least two distinct parts (Book 4, 439c-e).
