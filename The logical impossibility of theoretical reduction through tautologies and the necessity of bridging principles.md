Why is it logically impossible to deductively derive a biological law from physico-chemical principles alone, and what auxiliary premises are required to resolve this?
#### Notes
**Nature of Proposition: Logical Argument / Premise.** Deductive logic dictates that the consequences of a set of premises cannot contain any "new" terms that do not appear in the premises. Since physico-chemical laws contain no specifically biological terms, they cannot deductively yield biological laws on their own. To achieve deduction, the explanans must include **connecting laws** (bridge principles) that contain both physical and biological terms and assert necessary, sufficient, or necessary-and-sufficient physical conditions for biological features. (_Philosophy of Natural Science_, Chapter 8, pp. 103–104)
