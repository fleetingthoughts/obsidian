Map the ontological divisions of the "Divided Line," distinguishing the specific objects found within the visible and intelligible realms.
#### Notes
Socrates begins with the the intelligible section of the divided line:
**Argued Position.**<br>• _Premise_: The intelligible region is cut into two unequal segments representing two distinct cognitive methods: mathematical thought (_dianoia_) and dialectical intellection (_noesis_) (509d, 510b).<br>• _Argument_: It is argued that: (1) the lower segment (**thought** / _dianoia_) is used by geometers who treat starting assumptions as unquestioned "hypotheses," using visible drawings as images of the absolute entities they seek (like the square itself) and moving downward to a conclusion rather than upward to a beginning (510c–e); whereas (2) the higher segment (**intellection** / _noesis_) is grasped by **dialectic**, which treats hypotheses not as fixed beginnings, but strictly as stepping-stones and springboards to ascend to the unhypothetical beginning of the whole (the Good) (511b). Once it grasps this first principle, it descends back down to an end, making no use of any sensory images but moving strictly "through forms to forms, ending in forms" (Book 6, 510b–511c).

He then elaborates on the full mapping of the line
**Premise.** Socrates establishes the final epistemological mapping of the soul's states corresponding to the Divided Line's segments, ordered from highest (most clear/true) to lowest (most obscure/untrue): (1) **Intellection** (_noesis_) — corresponding to the highest intelligible segment (Dialectic grasping unhypothetical Forms) (511d); (2) **Thought** (_dianoia_) — corresponding to the lower intelligible segment (Geometry investigating hypotheses via sensory images) (511d); (3) **Trust** (_pistis_) — corresponding to the upper visible segment (actual physical objects, animals, and artifacts) (510a, 511e); and (4) **Imagination** (_eikasia_) — corresponding to the lowest visible segment (shadows, reflections in water, and copies of physical things) (509d–510a, 511e) (Book 6, 511d–e).
