How does Alfred Ayer refute the rationalist attempt to construct a deductive metaphysical system from self-evident first principles like Descartes' _Cogito_?
#### Notes
**Premise**: Rationalist metaphysics requires indubitable first principles that logically entail synthetic truths about reality.<br>**Analysis of Descartes**: Descartes' _Cogito_ reduces at most to the immediate proposition "there is a thought now." Its negation ("there is no thought now") is not logically self-contradictory.<br>**Refutation & Conclusion**: The existence of a continuous self ("I exist") does not logically follow from the occurrence of a single instantaneous thought. One isolated thought does not entail a persistent self, nor can pure deduction from immediate experience yield unobserved facts without relying on inductive rules. Therefore, a deductive synthetic metaphysical system is a logical impossibility. _(Chapter 2, pp. 46–47)_
