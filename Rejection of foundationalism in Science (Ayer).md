How does Alfred Ayer logically refute the rationalist attempt to construct a deductive metaphysical system from self-evident first principles, specifically through his critique of Descartes' _Cogito_?
#### Notes
**Nature of Propositions**: Refutation of Rationalist Foundationalism.<br><br>**Premise 1**: Rationalist metaphysics requires first principles that are logically certain (self-contradictory to deny) and non-empirical, yet capable of entailing factual truths about the universe.<br>**Premise 2**: Empirical generalizations (laws of nature) are fallible hypotheses and cannot serve as foundational first principles.<br>**Dialectical Counter-Argument (Descartes)**: Descartes attempts to base knowledge on _Cogito ergo sum_ ("I think, therefore I am"), treating "I think" as an indubitable first principle.<br>**Ayer's Refutation**:<br>1. _Analysis of Premise_: "Cogito" reduces at most to the immediate proposition "there is a thought now." Its negation ("there is no thought now") is not logically self-contradictory, because no significant synthetic proposition can be self-contradictory to deny.<br>2. _Logical Non-Sequitur (Unstated Assumption)_: "I exist" does not logically follow from "there is a thought now." The occurrence of an instantaneous thought at one moment does not entail that any other thought occurred previously, nor that a series of thoughts exists to constitute a single persisting self.<br>**Conclusion**: Pure deduction from immediate experience cannot yield unobserved factual knowledge without inductive general rules. Thus, a deductive synthetic metaphysical system is logically impossible.<br>**Citation**: Chapter 2, Pages 46–47.
