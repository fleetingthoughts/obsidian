What is the semantic difference between "statistical probability" and "logical/inductive probability" in scientific reasoning and what axioms do they both share
#### Notes
**Nature of Proposition: Conceptual Distinction.** Statistical probability represents a quantitative, empirical relation between repeatable kinds of events (an outcome $O$ and a random process $R$), roughly signifying their long-run relative frequency. Logical or inductive probability represents a non-empirical, quantitative logical relation between sentences, expressing the degree of evidential support or rational credibility that a set of premises (evidence $K$) lends to a hypothesis ($H$). (_Philosophy of Natural Science_, Chapter 5, p. 63)

They both share Kolmogorov's axioms.
