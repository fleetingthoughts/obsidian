In book 7, Socrates provides a curriculum and list of subjects that a philosopher must study:
- What is the motivation to study these subjects? With respect to [Plato's allegorical cave](Plato's%20allegorical%20cave.md), what do these subjects help the philosopher do in the analogy?
- What are the 5 subjects to be studied? What is the first of these subjects that is of utmost importance
- What analogy does Socrates give to outline the importance of the first subject to be studied? How does this justify the study of the subject?
- What are the two criteria that these studies satisfy for a sufficient education and how does each subject satisfy them?
- For each of these 5 subjects, Socrates also outlines how in practice they are wrongly studied in a way that does not satisfy his criteria for education. What are the mistakes people make in studying the subjects? 
- Glaucon also offers his idea of how one of these subjects should be studied. How does Socrates rebut it?
Finally, Socrates lays out the final subject to be studied. What is it?
