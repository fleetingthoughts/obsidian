Given a sequence $(a_n)$ and its subsequence $(a_{n_k})$, state what $k$ and $n_k$ represents? What can you say about the relationship between $n_k$ and $n$ if $k=n$.
#### Notes
Definitions of $k$ and $n_k$
$k$: This represents the index of the subsequence. It simply indicates the position of a term within the new, smaller sequence (e.g., $k=1$ is the first term of the subsequence, $k=2$ is the second term, etc.).
$n_k$: This represents the index of the original sequence. It tells you exactly where in the original sequence $(a_n)$ the $k$-th term of the subsequence was pulled from. The sequence of indices $(n_k)$ is a strictly increasing sequence of positive integers ($n_1 < n_2 < n_3 < \dots$).

The Relationship Between $n_k$ and $n$ (if $k = n$)If we evaluate the indices at $k = n$, the relationship is:$$n_k \geq n$$
