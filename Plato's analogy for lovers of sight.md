State Socrates' analogy for a lover of sights in contrast to a philosopher
#### Notes
**Premise.** Socrates establishes the analogical premise that dreaming (whether asleep or awake) consists in mistaking a likeness or copy of something for the actual thing itself to which it is like (e.g., seeing beautiful things but not beauty itself); conversely, being awake consists in recognizing both the Form itself and its participants without mistaking one for the other (Book 5, 476c–d).
