In his 2nd wave of arguments, Socrates explains the mechanics of his well-governed city for the purposes of unity and one aspect of it covers the strict rules on procreation:
- How does he justify his stance on procreation? Who's hobby does he specifically address to give a conventional modern practice as a point of comparison for his rules of procreation? 
- What limits does he impose on the procreation between men and women
- What happens to the procreations that do not abide the rules
#### Notes
- Socrates compares his rules on reproduction to Glaucon's socially acceptable selective dog breeding hobby (459a–b).
- Women and men are only allowed to procreate within the age limits of 20-40 and 30-55 (460e–461a)
- Children who are born without respecting the rules must be euthanized.
