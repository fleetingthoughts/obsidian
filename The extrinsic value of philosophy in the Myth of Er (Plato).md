How does Odysseus' choice of life in the Myth of Er demonstrate the practical value of his past suffering and philosophical wisdom? (Book 10, 620c–d)
#### Notes
**Argued Position.**<br>• _Premise_: Odysseus' soul draws the very last lot and goes to choose his next life (620c).<br>• _Argument_: Having recovered from the love of honor due to the memory of his former labors and sufferings, Odysseus goes around for a long time looking specifically for the **life of a private man who minds his own business** (620c–d). Upon finding it neglected on the ground, he gladly chooses it, stating he would have done the exact same thing even if he had drawn the first lot. This choice represents the ultimate triumph of philosophical prudence over the hollow, competitive desires for political power and public glory (Book 10, 620c–d).
