Why is it impossible to draw a sharp, absolute dividing line between hypotheses that are testable in principle and those that are not?
#### Notes
**Nature of Proposition: Epistemological Argument.** First, hypotheses normally yield test implications only when combined with suitable auxiliary assumptions, which are often tacit or change over time. Second, scientific ideas are often introduced in a vague, initial form with limited testability, and are only gradually refined into precise, diversely testable formulations. (_Philosophy of Natural Science_, Chapter 3, pp. 31-32)
