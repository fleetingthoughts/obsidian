What is the defining characteristic of the style of the sensible, virtuous speaker and what does he contrast it with
#### Notes
His style utilizes simple narrative for the vast majority of his speech, introducing only a small amount of imitation when reporting the steady and prudent deeds of a good man, while refusing to seriously imitate bad or inferior characters unless in play (Book 3, 396b–e).

Because human nature is minted in such small coins that a single person cannot practice multiple activities well, nor can they successfully imitate many different characters; trying to imitate diverse roles leads to failure in both the imitations and their primary social function (Book 3, 394e–395b). The common speaker relies almost entirely on physical and vocal imitation of all things (thunder, wind, animals, instruments), leaving very little room for simple narrative, which produces a highly chaotic and unstable rhetorical style (Book 3, 397a–b).
