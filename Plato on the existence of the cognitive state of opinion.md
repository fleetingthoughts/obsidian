How does Socrates logically map the three cognitive powers of the soul onto their respective ontological domains? How does he distinguish knowledge from opinion
#### Notes
**Argued Position.**<br>• _Premise_: Knowledge naturally depends on that which purely is (Being), while Ignorance necessarily depends on that which in no way is (Non-Being) (Book 5, 477a–b).<br>• _Argument_: Because there exists a third, intermediate state that both is and is not (Becoming), it is argued that there must be an intermediate cognitive power between knowledge and ignorance—which is **opinion**—that depends on this intermediate realm (Book 5, 477b–478d).

Socrates then specifically distinguishes knowledge from opinion:
**Argued Position.**<br>• _Premise_: Knowledge is an infallible power ("that which doesn't make mistakes"), whereas Opinion is a fallible power ("that which does make mistakes") (Book 5, 477e).<br>• _Argument_: Because two entirely different powers cannot naturally depend on the same thing or accomplish the same thing (Book 5, 478a–b), and because knowledge is infallible and opinion is fallible, it is argued that the knowable (Being) and the opinable (Becoming) must be entirely different ontological domains, meaning it is impossible to opine what one knows (Book 5, 478a–c).
