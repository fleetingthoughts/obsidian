If a "crucial test" is strictly impossible, what is its practical value, and how does Lenard's photoelectric experiment illustrate this distinction?
#### Notes
**Nature of Proposition: Historical Case Example.** While a crucial experiment cannot strictly prove or disprove a theory, it can be crucial in a practical sense by revealing a theory as seriously inadequate and directing subsequent research. In Lenard's photoelectric experiment, the non-continuous decrease of light energy as a screen moved away conflicted with classical wave theory but supported Einstein's photon theory, practically shifting the focus of optical research even though classical wave theory could theoretically be modified to fit the data. (_Philosophy of Natural Science_, Chapter 3, pp. 27-28)
