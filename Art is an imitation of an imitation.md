On what basis does Socrates argue that painting and visual imitation represent an imitation of looks (_phantasma_) rather than truth (_aletheia_)? (Book 10, 598a–b)
#### Notes
**Argued Position.**<br>• _Premise_: A physical object, such as a couch, looks different depending on the angle of observation (side, front, or top), yet the couch itself does not actually differ from itself or change its real nature (598a).<br>• _Argument_: Because painting is directed strictly toward representing how a thing looks from a single perspective rather than the actual physical object as it is, painting is an imitation of a superficial appearance (_looks_) rather than the truth. It only lays hold of a tiny, phantom-like part of the object (Book 10, 598a–b).
