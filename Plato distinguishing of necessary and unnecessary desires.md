How does Socrates define the distinction between "necessary" and "unnecessary" desires?
#### Notes
**Premise.** Socrates defines **necessary desires** as those we are naturally compelled to long for, which benefit us, and which we cannot turn aside justly (such as the desire for basic bread and relish for health); conversely, **unnecessary desires** are those of which a person can rid themselves if checked in youth, which do no good or cause harm to the body and soul with respect to prudence and moderation (such as the desire for luxurious foods, excess sex, and spendthrifty pleasures) (Book 8, 558d–559c).
