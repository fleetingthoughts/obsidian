Why does Hempel reject the neovitalistic explanation of biological directiveness (entelechies or vital forces) as a valid scientific theory and why Newton's theory of gravitation a good scientific theory
#### Notes
**Nature of Proposition: Epistemological Critique.** Neovitalism asserts that organic processes are directed by nonphysical agencies called entelechies. Hempel argues that this doctrine fails because its assumptions are too vague to permit the deductive derivation of any specific test implications. It does not specify under what circumstances entelechies go into action or how they will direct organic processes, rendering the concept testable only post factum and devoid of predictive or explanatory power. (_Philosophy of Natural Science_, Chapter 6, pp. 71-72)

**Nature of Proposition: Epistemological Argument.** The distinction does not lie in the materiality of the posited entities. Newton's theory is scientifically valid because it includes highly specific, mathematically precise assumptions (the law of gravitation and the laws of motion) that determine the exact forces physical bodies exert on one another and the resulting changes in their velocities. This precision allows for the deduction of definite test implications, enabling precise explanations, predictions (like Halley's comet), and retrodictions, which neovitalism cannot provide. (_Philosophy of Natural Science_, Chapter 6, p. 72)
