How does Socrates identify Civic Justice (_Dikaiosyne_) is the foundational virtue of the Kallipolis?
#### Notes
Socrates deduces justice by process of elimination as the last thing that is required for a well governed city
**Argued Position.**<br>• _Premise_: Justice is that which remains when wisdom, courage, and moderation have been discovered, acting as the power that enables them to come into being and preserves them once they have arisen (Book 4, 433b).<br>• _Argument_: Because the minding of one's own business (and not being a busybody) is the exact rule established at the city's founding to make the state good, it is argued that this practice of "one man, one job" is the ultimate definition of civic justice (Book 4, 432d-433b).

**Argued Position.**<br>• _Premise_: The exchange of simple tools between ordinary craftsmen (like a shoemaker and a carpenter) does not destroy the city (Book 4, 434a).<br>• _Argument_: Because the three distinct classes of the city must remain strictly bounded, it is argued that when a natural money-maker or warrior attempts to seize the deliberative office of the rulers through wealth or physical force, this meddling and exchange of class roles constitutes the greatest harm and "extreme evil-doing" against the state, which is the definition of injustice (Book 4, 434b-c).
