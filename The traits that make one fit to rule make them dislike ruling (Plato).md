Why is a city necessarily best governed when its prospective rulers are least eager to hold office?
#### Notes
**Argued Position.**<br>• _Premise_: Faction and civil war occur when politicians view public office as a private goldmine or a good to be seized for personal gain (Book 7, 521a).<br>• _Argument_: Because true philosophers possess a superior life that completely despises political office, it is argued that they will approach ruling strictly as a necessary duty rather than an object of desire; this prevents rival political factions and produces a city that is governed in the most stable and freest possible manner (Book 7, 520d–521b).
