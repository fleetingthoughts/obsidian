Why is it logically impossible to define every term in a scientific or mathematical system, and what is the necessary alternative?
#### Notes
**Nature of Proposition: Logical Argument.** If we attempted to define every term, we would either fall into circularity (defining a term with the help of some of its predecessors in the chain, which fails to avoid the defined word) or an infinite regress (where our understanding of one term depends on the next indefinitely, with the result that no term is ever explained). To escape this, a system must rely on a set of **primitive terms** that receive no definitions within the system but serve as the bedrock for defining all other terms. (_Philosophy of Natural Science_, Chapter 7, pp. 86–87)
