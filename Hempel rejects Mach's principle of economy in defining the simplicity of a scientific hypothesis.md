Why does Hempel reject Ernst Mach's and Karl Pearson's "principle of economy" (parsimony) as a sufficient epistemological justification for preferring simpler hypotheses?
#### Notes
**Nature of Proposition: Epistemological Critique.** The principle of economy successfully justifies simplicity when we only choose between different mathematical descriptions of a single, already-known set of facts. However, in science, adopting a general hypothesis means adopting its predictions for untested cases; competing hypotheses that fit the same past data (e.g., three different polynomial curves) make widely different predictions for future cases, meaning simplicity cannot be justified purely on the grounds of descriptive economy. (_Philosophy of Natural Science_, Chapter 4, pp. 41-42)
