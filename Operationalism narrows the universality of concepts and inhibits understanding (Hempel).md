How can accepted scientific theories show the inaccuracy of the very operational criteria originally used to test them, and what metaphor does Hempel use to describe this?
#### Notes
**Nature of Proposition: Epistemological Argument.** Hempel compares this process to **building a bridge on temporary supports (pontoons)**: we initially use crude operational criteria (temporary supports like sundials or the axial rotation of Earth for time) to gather data and formulate laws. Once the system of laws is accepted, its principles show that those original criteria are only approximately accurate (e.g., tidal friction slowly decelerates Earth's rotation), leading us to shift the foundations and adopt more precise criteria (quartz or atomic clocks) to refine the total system. (_Philosophy of Natural Science_, Chapter 7, pp. 95–96)
