How does Hempel formulate the debate concerning whether the chronological order of discovery affects the confirmation of a hypothesis (the "new evidence" problem)?
#### Notes
**Nature of Proposition: Epistemological Argument.** One view holds that fitting "new" cases (not known during formulation) provides stronger confirmation because any finite quantitative data can be artificially fitted by some complex hypothesis. Hempel's preferred logical view holds that the logical relation of confirmation depends solely on what the hypothesis asserts and what the data are; the chronological order is a historical accident that does not affect the timeless logical support the data lend to the hypothesis. (_Philosophy of Natural Science_, Chapter 4, pp. 37-38)
