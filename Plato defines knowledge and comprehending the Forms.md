How does Socrates use the ontological status of physical particulars versus the Forms to logically prove that knowledge (_episteme_) is strictly and exclusively the apprehension of the Forms? (Book 5, 479a–480a)
#### Notes
Knowledge is initially defined as knowing the forms, but to provide a more primitive argument, he appeals to the "power (dynamis)" of knowledge and we establish:
- First, he defines **powers** (_dynameis_) generally as things defined by **what they depend on** (their objects) and **what they accomplish**.
- Second, he gets the opponent to agree that **knowledge is an infallible power** ("that which doesn't make mistakes"), while **opinion is fallible** ("that which does make mistakes").
- Third, he establishes the foundational definition: **"Knowledge is presumably dependent on what is, to know of what is that it is and how it is"** (knowledge is the power directed at absolute, unchanging Being).

At this stage in the argument, **the word "Form" has not even been used to define the object of knowledge.** The definition is simply: _Knowledge is the infallible grasp of what purely is_.

4. The core of the argument (478b–480a)

Now that the general definitions are set, Socrates must **argue** to prove which objects in the universe actually satisfy these definitions:

1. **Premise**: Because knowledge (infallible) and opinion (fallible) are different powers, they **cannot depend on the same objects**.
2. **Premise**: Physical particulars (like "many beautiful things" or "many doubles") are fundamentally contradictory; a beautiful painting can look ugly in a different light, and a double unit is also a half compared to something else. They both "are and are not" simultaneously.
3. **Deduction**: Because physical particulars are unstable and contradictory, they do not qualify as "what purely is" (pure Being). Therefore, **it is logically impossible to "know" them** under the definition of knowledge. They can only be the objects of **opinion**.
4. **Deduction**: The only entities that "always stay the same in all respects" without admitting of their opposites are the **Forms** (such as Beauty itself or Justice itself).
5. **Conclusion**: Since the Forms are the only things that purely _are_, and knowledge is defined as the grasp of _what is_, **knowledge is strictly and exclusively the understanding of the Forms**.

When Socrates creates the dichotomy of the forms (e.g. Beauty and Ugly; Justice and Injustice). To know about these is to know what "is". When he defines ignorance as knowing nothing, he is referring to the absence and knowing nothing. I do not think Socrates is saying that knowing Ugly or Injustice is the same as ignorance.
