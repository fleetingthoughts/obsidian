How does logical analysis dissolve the apparently necessary physical fact that "a material thing cannot be in two places at once"?
#### Notes
**Nature of Propositions**: Grammatical Illusion & Analysis of Linguistic Convention.<br><br>**Surface Appearance**: The statement appears to be a synthetic empirical proposition about physical objects that possesses absolute logical certainty.<br>**Logical Analysis**:<br>1. _Premise 1_: No synthetic empirical proposition can ever be logically necessary or certain.<br>2. _Premise 2_: The statement records a linguistic convention regarding the definition of the symbol "material thing."<br>3. _Deductive Breakdown_: The convention dictates that if two sense-contents occur simultaneously in the same visual or tactual spatial field, they are defined as belonging to different material objects.<br>**Conclusion**: The necessity of the proposition is purely linguistic, recording our determination to use words in a specific way. Altering our verbal conventions would alter its truth value without changing physical reality.<br>**Citation**: Chapter 2, Pages 57–58.
