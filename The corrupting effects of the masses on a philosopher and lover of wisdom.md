According to Socrates, how does the mass in a democracy enforce the societal mechanisms (492b) that favor specific skills (492d-493d)? What skills are favored?

What does Socrates call this person that possesses the skills that are favored and describe this person. 

What premise does Socrates assert about the masses' ability to know forms (493e) and what does that say about the kind of [cognitive faculties.](Plato's%20description%20of%20the%20forms.md) that are favored.
#### Notes
- **Societal Mechanisms (492b–493d):** The masses enforce their will when they gather in assemblies, courts, or theaters, using overwhelming "shouting and clapping" to praise or blame. Socrates says the private wage earners (sophists) simply learn the "angers and desires of a great, strong beast" (the multitude) and call the act of catering to the beast's moods "wisdom.
- **The Masses and the Forms (493e–494a):** Socrates asserts that it is impossible for the multitude to believe that "the fair itself" (the Form) exists; they only believe in the "many fair things". Therefore, it is "impossible that a multitude be philosophic". This aligns perfectly with his Book V definition: the masses are restricted purely to the cognitive faculty of Opinion, as they cannot grasp absolute knowledge.
