What is the final metaphysical argument the Laws present regarding the consequences of Socrates' decision in the afterlife?
#### Notes
**Nature of Proposition**: Metaphysical / Eschatological Premise. **Philosophical Concept**: Divine Command Theory / Cosmic Justice. **Inferential Logic**: The Laws argue that they are sisters to the Laws of the Underworld (Hades). If Socrates departs this life having unjustly escaped, the Laws of the Underworld will not receive him kindly, knowing he tried to destroy their earthly counterparts. Conversely, if he dies in obedience to the court, he departs as a victim of human injustice, leaving his moral integrity intact before the divine judges. **Citation**: _Crito_, 54b–c [Passage 10].
