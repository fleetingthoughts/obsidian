What does Socrates criticize of Homer's depiction of Achilles and why
#### Notes
Homer portrays Achilles as both a greedy lover of money (willing to hold back his wrath or release a corpse only for ransom) and an arrogant, impious rebel against the gods; Socrates argues that these two moral defects are incompatible with his noble lineage (grandson of Peleus, student of Chiron) and must be rejected as false (Book 3, 391a–c).

Showing divine figures as susceptible to gifts (e.g., "gifts persuade gods") breeds greed (_philochrematía_) and moral corruption in the young, who will believe that unjust actions can be easily expiated through material offerings (Book 3, 390e–391a).
