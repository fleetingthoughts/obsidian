How does Hempel's analysis of mathematical proofs illustrate that even purely deductive disciplines depend on creative imagination rather than mechanical rules?
#### Notes
**Nature of Proposition: Methodological Analogy.** The rules of deductive inference specify an infinity of valid conclusions from any premise (e.g., "p" implies "p or q" for any statement q), meaning they provide no direction for finding interesting theorems or constructing proofs. Discovering mathematical theorems requires inventive ingenuity; the deductive rules serve only as a modest, mechanical criterion for verifying if a proposed proof is sound. (_Philosophy of Natural Science_, Chapter 2, pp. 15–16)
