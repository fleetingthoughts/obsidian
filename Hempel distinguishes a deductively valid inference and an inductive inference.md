How does Hempel distinguish between a deductively valid inference and an inductive inference in terms of the logical strength of their conclusions?
#### Notes
**Nature of Proposition: Conceptual Distinction.** In a deductively valid inference, the premises imply the conclusion with absolute certainty; if the premises are true, the conclusion cannot fail to be true. In an inductive inference, the premises (concerning particular cases) imply the general conclusion only with a more or less high probability; the conclusion may still be false even if all observed premises are true. (_Philosophy of Natural Science_, Chapter 2, pp. 9–10)
