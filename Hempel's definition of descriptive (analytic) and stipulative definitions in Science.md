How does Hempel distinguish between descriptive (analytic) and stipulative definitions in scientific and philosophical contexts?
#### Notes
**Nature of Proposition: Conceptual Distinction.** A descriptive definition describes the accepted meaning of a term already in use and can be judged as more or less accurate, and thus true or false (e.g., 'father' means 'male parent'). A stipulative definition assigns a special meaning to a term by convention—whether newly coined (e.g., 'pi-meson') or an old term used technically (e.g., 'strangeness')—and because it functions as a convention, it cannot be qualified as true or false. (_Philosophy of Natural Science_, Chapter 7, pp. 85–86)
