The relational analogy of the "Changeling Child" demonstrating the severe sociological danger of introducing dialectic to the young.
#### Notes
- **The Changeling Child:** The young student, raised securely with ancestral convictions about what is just and fair (538c).
    
- **The Discovery of Adoption:** The student experiences refutation in dialectical argument, realizing his ancestral laws are not absolute truth (538d-e).
    
- **The Flatterers:** Opposing, pleasure-seeking practices that draw the destabilized soul toward them (538d).
    
- **Conclusion:** Deprived of his old convictions and lacking true knowledge, the young man becomes an outlaw, treating arguments like a game to tear at others like a puppy, bringing slander upon philosophy (539a-c)
