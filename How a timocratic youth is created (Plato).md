How is the Timocratic youth psychologically generated from his domestic environment?
#### Notes
**Argued Position.**<br>• _Premise_: A youth's character is shaped by the competing influences of his family and society.<br>• _Argument_: When a boy listens to his mother complain that her husband is not a ruler, is not serious about money, and is held in small account because he avoids civic busybodiness, and when domestics urge the boy to be "more of a man" than his father by prosecuting debtors, his soul is pulled in opposite directions. The father waters the calculating part of his soul, while the mother and domestics water the desiring and spirited parts. It is argued that the youth compromises by turning over internal rule to the spirited part, becoming a haughty-minded honor-lover (Book 8, 549c–550b).
