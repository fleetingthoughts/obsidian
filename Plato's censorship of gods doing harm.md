What foundational premises does Socrates make regarding the Goodness of God and its implications with respect to literature
#### Notes
The two premises are as follows:
- God is good and following his Book 1 argument: good cannot do harm.
This results in censorship of common tropes in literature to prevent corruption of the youth:

**The First Theological Model / Law: God is Cause Only of Good (379a–380c)**

- **Premise:** God is good and not harmful (379b). What is not harmful does no evil and cannot be the cause of any evil (379b).
- **Argument:** God is not the cause of everything, but the cause of a few good things for human beings, while not responsible for bad things (379c).
- **Censorship Rule:** Reject Homeric claims that Zeus dispenses good and evil from two jars (379d–e), or that divine forces caused Pandarus' oath-breaking or god-strife (379e–380a). Poets describing divine punishment must state that the god's works were just and good, and that the bad men profited by being punished (380a–b).
