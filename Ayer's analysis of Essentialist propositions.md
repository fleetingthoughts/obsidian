What is Ayer's general rule for interpreting traditional philosophical questions of the form "What is X?" (such as "What is a material object?" or "What is a number?")?
#### Notes
**Nature of Propositions**: Methodological Rule of Analysis & Elimination of Essentialism.<br><br>**Traditional Metaphysical Error**: Treating questions like "What is a material object?" or "What is a number?" as searches for the unobservable essence of real entities.<br>**Methodological Rule**: All philosophical questions of the form "What is X?" are strictly requests for a definition in use of the symbol "X".<br>**Definition in Use**: Providing a definition in use of "X" means demonstrating how all sentences containing the symbol "X" can be systematically translated into equivalent sentences containing symbols of a lower or more direct logical type (such as translating sentences containing "material object" into sentences referring to sense-contents).<br>**Conclusion**: Philosophy resolves essentialist queries by revealing the hidden logical complexity of sentences through linguistic translation, dispelling metaphysical pseudo-problems.<br>**Citation**: Chapter 2, Pages 58–60.
