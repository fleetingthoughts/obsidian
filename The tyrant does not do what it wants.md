Why does the tyrannic soul, despite its absolute license, "least do what it wants" when viewed as a whole?
#### Notes
**Argued Position.**<br>• _Premise_: Doing what one wants requires a unified, rational will rather than being dragged by erratic impulses (Book 9, 577d–e).<br>• _Argument_: Because the tyrannic soul is ruled by a small, mad desire that acts as a tyrant, the entire soul is constantly and forcibly drawn by a gadfly. This results in a mind that is perpetually full of internal faction, confusion, and regret, meaning that when viewed as a whole composite entity, it is logically incapable of doing what it truly wants (Book 9, 577e).
