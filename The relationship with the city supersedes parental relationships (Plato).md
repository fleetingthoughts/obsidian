How do the Laws of Athens argue for an asymmetric moral relationship between the citizen and the state using the parental analogy?
#### Notes
**Nature of Proposition**: Analogy / Premise. **Philosophical Concept**: Filial Piety / Political Paternalism / Asymmetric Obligation. **Inferential Logic**: The Laws argue that because they brought Socrates' parents together in marriage, brought him to birth, and nurtured and educated him, Socrates is their "offspring and servant." Consequently, he does not stand on an equal footing with them. Just as a child has no right to strike back or retaliate against a parent, a citizen has no right to retaliate against or destroy their country and its laws when they are punished. **Citation**: _Crito_, 50c–51c [Passage 7, 8].
