How does Alfred Ayer logically define a "metaphysical sentence," and how does this relate to his division of all significant propositions?
#### Notes
**Logical Definition and Partition of Cognitive Meaning**: Exhaustive categorization of meaningful statements (Hume's Fork).<br><br>**Ayer's Exhaustive Partition**: Following David Hume's fork, all significant propositions are divided into two mutually exclusive and collectively exhaustive classes:<br>1. _Analytic propositions (Tautologies)_: Formally certain truths of logic and pure mathematics, which say nothing about matters of fact but record our determination to use symbols in a certain way.<br>2. _Empirical hypotheses (Synthetic propositions)_: Factual statements whose validity is determined solely by experience and probability.<br><br>**Definition of Metaphysical Sentence**: A sentence is metaphysical if it purports to express a genuine proposition but, in fact, expresses neither a tautology nor an empirical hypothesis.<br><br>**Logical Conclusion**: Since tautologies and empirical hypotheses exhaust the entire domain of significant propositions, all metaphysical assertions are logically nonsensical (devoid of literal significance).<br><br>**Citation**: Chapter 1, Page 41.
