Why does a series of favorable test outcomes fail to provide a deductively conclusive proof of a scientific hypothesis?
#### Notes
**Nature of Proposition: Formal Logical Fallacy.** The reasoning of confirmation takes the form: "If H is true, then so is I; I is true; therefore, H is true." This commits the Fallacy of Affirming the Consequent, which is deductively invalid; a hypothesis can yield true test implications and still be false, as demonstrated by the first version of Semmelweis's hypothesis which falsely claimed cadaveric matter was the _only_ source of infection. (_Philosophy of Natural Science_, Chapter 2, pp. 6–7)
