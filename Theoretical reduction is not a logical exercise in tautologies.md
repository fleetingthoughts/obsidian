Why does Hempel reject the idea that reducing biological terms to physico-chemical ones requires showing that they are "synonymous" (intensionally identical), and what does he propose instead?
#### Notes
**Nature of Proposition: Epistemological Critique / Alternative Premise.** Hempel argues that it is preposterous to require biological terms to have physico-chemical synonyms because no biological term (e.g., 'goose egg', 'mitosis') has the same meaning as a physical or chemical expression. Instead, he proposes that reduction requires only an **extensional definition**, where the physico-chemical expression specifies conditions that, as a matter of empirical fact, are satisfied by all and only those instances to which the biological term applies. (_Philosophy of Natural Science_, Chapter 8, p. 102)
