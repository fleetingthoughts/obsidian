To escape the contradiction of fallible rulers making bad laws, how does Thrasymachus redefine the ruler?
#### Notes
He introduces the ruler "in the precise sense," who never makes mistakes and strictly sets down what is best for himself (340e–341a).

He argues that a doctor, a calculator, or a ruler is only such insofar as he does not err (Book 1, 340d–e). In "precise speech," a craftsman only makes a mistake when his knowledge fails him, at which point he is not practicing his craft (Book 1, 340e). Therefore, a ruler, insofar as he is a ruler in the precise sense, never makes a mistake and always commands what is best for himself (Book 1, 340e–341a).
