How does Alfred Ayer unravel the pseudo-factual statement "Relations are not particulars, but universals," and what does it reveal about philosophical questions regarding abstract entities?
#### Notes
**Nature of Propositions**: Category Analysis & Translation of Pseudo-Ontological Claims.<br><br>**Surface Illusion**: "Relations are universals" looks like an empirical classification statement analogous to "Armenians are Christians."<br>**Logical Analysis**:<br>1. _Premise 1_: An empirical statement like "Armenians are Christians" describes the observed characteristics of a group of physical entities.<br>2. _Premise 2_: "Relations are universals" does not describe real non-empirical objects called "relations" or "universals."<br>3. _Analytical Translation_: It is a linguistic rule recording the convention that relation-symbols belong by definition to the syntactic class of symbols for attributes, rather than the class of symbols for individual things.<br>**Conclusion**: Inquiries into universals or abstract entities are not investigations into unobservable objects, but requests for definitions of symbols and rules governing symbolic classification.<br>**Citation**: Chapter 2, Pages 58–59.
