Socrates had argued how those with innate talents are more easily but corruptible but how does Socrates argue that the very virtues of a philosophic nature can lead to its own moral ruin?
#### Notes
**Argued Position.**<br>• _Premise_: External "goods" like wealth, beauty, physical strength, and influential relatives corrupt and distract a young person (491c, 495a).<br>• _Argument_: Because an exceptionally gifted youth (possessing memory, quickness, and courage) will be flattered, fawned over, and pressured by citizens wanting to exploit their future power (494b–c), the youth will develop empty conceit and pretension (494c–d). If someone gently tells them they lack intelligence and must work to acquire it, their corrupting environment will actively prevent them from listening, meaning their very capacity for greatness facilitates their corruption (Book 6, 494c–495a).
