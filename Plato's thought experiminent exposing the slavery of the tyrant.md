How does Socrates' "slave-owner in the desert" thought experiment expose the paralyzing fear of the political tyrant?
#### Notes
**Argued Position.**<br>• _Premise_: Private citizens who own many slaves are secure because the entire city's legal structure defends them (Book 9, 578d).<br>• _Argument_: If a god were to lift a wealthy man with fifty or more slaves out of the city and deposit him, his family, and his property in a desert place where no free men could help him, the owner would live in extreme, paralyzing fear of destruction by his own domestics. He would be compelled to flatter his own servants, promise them freedom, and become a cringing servant to his own slaves to survive (Book 9, 578e–579b).
