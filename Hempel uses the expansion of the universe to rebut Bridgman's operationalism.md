How does Hempel refute Bridgman's claim that the hypothesis of a universal, undetectable doubling of all distances every 24 hours is "operationally meaningless"?
#### Notes
**Nature of Proposition: Epistemological Argument.** Bridgman declared the doubling hypothesis meaningless because our measuring rods would also double, making detection impossible. Hempel refutes this by arguing that a hypothesis must be evaluated within its systematic context, where it is conjoined with other physical laws acting as auxiliary hypotheses; for instance, if distances double, the travel time of sound waves across a lake should also double, or if wave velocities also double, stellar surface areas would quadruple, reducing solar brightness to (\frac{1}{4}), both of which yield test implications. (_Philosophy of Natural Science_, Chapter 7, pp. 96–97
