After explaining his model of a well-governed city and the corruptions that threaten a philosophic, walk through the hypothetical scenario he explains in which such a city can be formed from an existing one (502a-504e):
- What does Socrates think about the current cities' ability to preserve the philosopher
- What does he say about the timeline of the youth's education. When should they practice philosophy (498a-498b)?
- How does the philosopher king come to be and what is the first and foremost priority? What difficulty does he highlight that relates to the nature of the philosophic soul (503b-503e).

Finally, how does this lead to his next proposition about the nature of the Good.
#### Notes

**The Formation of the City:** While highly unlikely, it is not impossible for a true philosopher to come to power by chance. To form the ideal city from an existing one, the philosopher-king must wipe the slate clean. They would exile the adult population and take over the education of the children to protect them from existing societal corruption. The philosophic soul is naturally quick to learnbut must also be composed to preserve their values. These conflicting natures makes their education difficult.

**The Transition to the Good:** Because the philosopher-king is responsible for ordering this new city perfectly, they must undergo the "greatest study" (_megiston mathema_). Knowing justice and moderation is useless without understanding the Form of the Good, which is wh
at actually makes justice beneficial. Therefore, Socrates must now define the ultimate goal of their education: The Good itself.
