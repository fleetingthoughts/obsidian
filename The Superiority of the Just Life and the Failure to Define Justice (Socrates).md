Using the established definitions of the soul's work and virtue, how does Socrates logically deduce that the just man is happier, and why does this end in _aporia_?
#### Notes
Because a just soul has the proper virtue, it performs its work well and lives a happy life, proving injustice is never more profitable (353e–354a). However, it ends in _aporia_ because Socrates admits they debated the qualities of justice without ever defining the _essence_ of what justice actually is (354b–c).
