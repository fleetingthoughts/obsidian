How does Eddington use the "two tables" thought experiment to argue for extreme scientific realism, and why does Hempel reject his conclusion?
#### Notes
**Nature of Proposition: Epistemological Critique.** Eddington argues that modern physics reveals that only his "scientific table" (mostly empty space with rushing electric charges) is really there, while the "familiar table" is a mere mental illusion. Hempel rejects this, arguing that to explain a macroscopic phenomenon is not to explain it away. The atomic theory does not show a solid table is not there; it takes its macroscopic properties (solidity, extension) for granted and seeks to show in virtue of what microprocesses it displays those characteristics, as evidenced by the bridge principles' explicit reference to macroproperties. (_Philosophy of Natural Science_, Chapter 6, pp. 77-78)
