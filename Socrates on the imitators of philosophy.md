Socrates defends philosophy against accusations of uselessness and wickedness from Glaucon by addressing two points:
- How the many corrupt the philosophic soul.
- What causes philosophy to be filled with incompetent imitators.
Elaborate on the 2nd point:
- What allows the imitators to take up philosophy (495a-c, 496a-e) and why do they do so? 
- How does this scenario affect the masses' perception of people who actually practice philosophy or supposedly practice philosophy?  
- What analogy does he make to support this?
