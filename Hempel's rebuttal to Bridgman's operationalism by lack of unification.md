What is Bridgman's Maxim regarding different operational procedures (e.g., tactual vs. optical length), and why does Hempel reject it?
#### Notes
**Nature of Proposition: Epistemological Critique.** Bridgman's Maxim states that different operational procedures must be regarded as characterizing different, distinct concepts (such as distinguishing 'tactual length' from 'optical length') because treating them as the same concept is empirically unsafe. Hempel rejects this because it leads to a theoretically endless and practically unmanageable proliferation of scientific concepts—obliging us to distinguish different concepts for every minor variation in measuring apparatus, which defeats the core scientific goal of systematic unification. (_Philosophy of Natural Science_, Chapter 7, pp. 91–94
