Describe the Ring of Gyges analogy and the motivation for this analogy
#### Notes
- **Premise:** Human nature naturally pursues self-interest as good, whereas law forcibly perverts it to honor equality (359c).
- **Argument / Thought Experiment (Ring of Gyges):** An ancestor of Gyges finds a hollow bronze horse in a chasm containing a corpse wearing a gold ring (359d–e). Turning the collet of the ring inward makes him invisible; turning it outward makes him visible (359e–360a). He uses this power to commit adultery with the king's wife, murder the king, and take over rule (360a–b).
- **Conclusion / Argument:** If two such rings existed and were given to a "just" man and an "unjust" man, both would go the same way once shielded from consequences (360b–c). This proves no one is willingly just, but only when compelled to be so (360c–d).
