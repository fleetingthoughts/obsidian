What is the instrumentalist argument against the reality of theoretical entities based on the "lack of full definition," and how does Hempel refute it?
#### Notes
**Nature of Proposition: Epistemological Argument.** The instrumentalist argues that because theoretical terms cannot be fully defined using antecedently understood concepts, they lack definite meaning, rendering theoretical statements neither true nor false but merely convenient symbolic calculation devices. Hempel refutes this by arguing that a concept can be used precisely and objectively with only a partial specification of meaning. For example, temperature is precisely used within the limits of a mercury thermometer without a universal definition, and bridge principles provide valid, objective, partial criteria for using theoretical terms. (_Philosophy of Natural Science_, Chapter 6, pp. 78-79)
