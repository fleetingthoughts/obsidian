State 4 foundational premises that necessitates the inception of a city and what specific societal mechanisms or features are a consequence of these premises.
#### Notes
**Foundational Premises**
1. **Human Non-Self-Sufficiency:** A city comes into being because each individual is not self-sufficient but in need of many things (369b).
2. **Natural Aptitude / Specialization:** Each person is naturally not quite like anyone else, but differs in nature, making different men apt for different jobs (370a–b).
3. **One Man, One Art:** A person does a finer job practicing one art rather than many arts (370b).
4. **Timeliness:** Work cannot await the leisure of the worker; the worker must follow close upon the thing done (370b–c).

**Development and Structure of the City**

- **Basic Needs (4–5 Men):** Provision of food, housing, clothing, shoes, and body care (369d–e).
- **Division of Labor:** Each worker puts his work at the disposition of all in common (369e–370a).
- **Expansion:** Carpenters, smiths, craftsmen, herdsmen, merchants, sea traders, market tradesmen (whose bodies are weakest), and wage earners (selling physical strength) are added (370c–371e).
- **Life in the Simple City:** Citizens feast on barley meal and wheat flour, drink wine, wear wreaths, and refrain from producing children beyond their means to prevent poverty or war (371e–372c)
