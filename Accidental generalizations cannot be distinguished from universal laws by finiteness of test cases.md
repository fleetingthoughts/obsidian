How does Hempel disprove the idea that the difference between laws and accidental generalizations is simply that the latter can be reduced to a finite conjunction of singular statements?
#### Notes
**Nature of Proposition: Epistemological Critique.** This distinction is inadequate on two grounds: first, the statement "all rocks in this box contain iron" does not specify the number of rocks or name them, meaning it is not logically equivalent to a finite conjunction without adding external counting information. Second, the accidental generalization "all bodies of pure gold have a mass of less than 100,000 kilograms" would remain an accidental generalization even if there were infinitely many bodies of gold in the universe
