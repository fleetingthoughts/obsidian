Why does Ayer reject the view that the metaphysician is equivalent to a poet on the ground that both "write nonsense"? Explain his distinction between scientific and emotive uses of language.
#### Notes
**Semantic Distinction of Linguistic Functions**: Factual vs. emotive discourse.<br><br>**The Myth**: Critics argue that metaphysicians are merely poets who express aesthetic or moral visions through literal nonsense.<br><br>**Ayer's Distinction (Premises & Argument)**:<br>1. _Premise 1_: The sentences produced by poets are rarely literal nonsense; they almost always possess literal meaning and express genuine, although often false, propositions (e.g., metaphors still describe states of affairs).<br>2. _Premise 2_: The difference between the scientific use of language and the emotive/poetic use is not that one has sense and the other has none, but that the scientist is primarily concerned with expressing _true propositions_, whereas the poet is concerned with the _expression of feelings_ and the _creation of art_.<br>3. _Conclusion_: The metaphysician does not intend to write poetry or emotive nonsense; they intend to express genuine factual hypotheses. They lapse into literal nonsense unconsciously, duped by grammar and committing logical errors of reasoning. Thus, metaphysics is merely a product of humdrum logical mistakes, not creative art.<br><br>**Citation**: Chapter 1, Pages 44–45.
