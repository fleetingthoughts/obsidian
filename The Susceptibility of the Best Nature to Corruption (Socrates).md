Explain the paradox Socrates presents regarding the corruption of the highest philosophic nature by traditional "goods" and bad rearing.
#### Notes
The most vigorous seeds or natures become exceptionally deficient when subjected to unsuitable nourishment or environments, meaning the best nature comes off worse from bad rearing than an ordinary one (491d). Consequently, the very virtues of the philosopher (courage, moderation) and external goods (wealth, physical strength, powerful relatives) actively corrupt the soul and tear it away from philosophy when guided by a flawed education (491b–c).
