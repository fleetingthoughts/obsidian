How does Socrates use the comparison of "like and unlike" in the crafts to show that the just man is wise and good, while the unjust man is bad and unlearned?
#### Notes
He establishes that a musical man tuning a lyre or a doctor prescribing food only seeks to outdo (get the better of) the unmusical or non-medical man (their unlikes), not their fellow experts (their likes) (Book 1, 349e–350a). In contrast, the ignorant man tries to outdo both the knowledgeable and the ignorant (both like and unlike) (Book 1, 350b). Since the unjust man tries to outdo both the just and the unjust, while the just man only tries to outdo the unjust, the just man behaves like the wise and good, while the unjust man behaves like the ignorant and bad (Book 1, 350b–d).
