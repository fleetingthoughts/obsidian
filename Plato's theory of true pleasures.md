How does Socrates use the concept of "fullness" (_plerosis_) to establish the ontological superiority of intellectual pleasures over physical ones?
#### Notes
**rgued Position.**<br>• _Premise_: True fullness is filling an emptiness with that which has more genuine Being and Truth (Book 9, 585a–b).<br>• _Argument_: Physical hunger is an emptiness of the body filled by food and drink, which are mortal and constantly changing. Ignorance is an emptiness of the soul filled by true opinion, knowledge, and virtue, which are immortal and always the same (585a–c). Because the soul and the objects of intellect (the Forms) participate infinitely more in pure Being and Truth than the body and its food, it is argued that the soul experiences a truer, more real fullness, resulting in a pleasure that is infinitely more trustworthy and true (Book 9, 585d–e).
