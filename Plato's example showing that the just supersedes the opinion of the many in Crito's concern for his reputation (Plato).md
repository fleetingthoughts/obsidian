What is Crito's primary argument concerning reputation (the opinion of the many), and how does Socrates logically subvert it?
#### Notes
**Nature of Proposition**: Dialectical Counter-Argument / Premise. **Philosophical Concept**: Social Reputation vs. Objective Virtue. **Inferential Logic**: Crito argues that if Socrates dies, the public will believe Crito valued his money more than his friend's life, bringing shame and a reputation of cowardice upon Socrates' friends. Socrates subverts this by arguing that the opinions of the majority are irrelevant because they cannot inflict either the greatest evils (making a man foolish) or the greatest goods (making a man wise), and that only the opinions of the wise (the expert) matter. **Citation**: _Crito_, 44c–d [Passage 2].
