State how Glaucon models the conception of justice and his motivation for doing so
#### Notes
**Part 1: The Origin and Nature of Justice (Social Contract) (358e–359b)**
- **Premise:** By nature, doing injustice is good, while suffering injustice is bad. However, the badness of suffering injustice far exceeds the good in doing it (358e).
- **Argument:** When humans taste both doing and suffering injustice without being able to escape the one and choose the other, they set down a compact neither to do nor suffer it (358e–359a). Laws and compacts arise from this compromise; justice is a mean between what is best (doing injustice without paying the penalty) and what is worst (suffering injustice without being able to avenge oneself) (359a).
- **Premise:** Justice is cared for not as an intrinsic good, but because it is honored due to a want of vigor in doing injustice (359a–b).
