Why are bridge principles logically indispensable for both the explanatory power and the testability of a scientific theory?
#### Notes
**Nature of Proposition: Logical Premise.** The internal principles of a theory are formulated in terms of novel, unobservable theoretical concepts (such as electron jumps and energy levels). Because these concepts refer to entities we do not know how to directly measure or observe, the internal principles alone cannot yield any test implications. To be testable and explanatory, the theory must deductively derive implications formulated in "antecedently understood" or pretheoretical terms (such as emission spectrum and wavelength). This derivation is logically impossible without bridge principles to connect the theoretical terms to the pretheoretical vocabulary. (_Philosophy of Natural Science_, Chapter 6, pp. 73-74)
