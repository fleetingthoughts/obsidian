How does the application of the verification criterion reveal that the traditional debate between monists and pluralists regarding "substance" is a fictitious dispute?
#### Notes
**Metaphilosophical Application of the Verifiability Principle**: Critique of speculative ontology.<br><br>**The Issue**: Monists assert that reality is ultimately one substance, while pluralists assert it is many.<br><br>**Analysis (Premises & Deduction)**:<br>1. _Premise 1_: For a dispute to be genuine and factually significant, there must be some conceivable empirical observation that would be relevant to determining the truth or falsehood of the competing assertions.<br>2. _Premise 2_: Both monists and pluralists admit that no empirical situation can possibly be imagined that would resolve, or even affect the probability of, their dispute; the sensory world remains identical whether reality is "one substance" or "many."<br>3. _Conclusion_: The assertions "reality is one substance" and "reality is many substances" are entirely devoid of factual content. The metaphysical question of substance is thus a spurious, fictitious problem.<br><br>**Citation**: Chapter 1, Pages 39–40.
