Why does Socrates wait until Book 10 to "give back" the wages, honors, and reputations of justice that were stripped away in Book 2? (Book 10, 612b–614a)
#### Notes
**Argued Position.**<br>• _Premise_: To answer Glaucon's challenge purely, justice had to be evaluated _all alone by itself_, assuming the just man had the reputation of absolute villainy and the unjust man had the reputation of a saint (612b–c).<br>• _Argument_: Having successfully proven in Book 9 that justice in itself is inherently best for the soul's internal constitution, Socrates argues it is now safe to return the "wages and reputations" because they have established the intrinsic truth. He demonstrates that in reality, the just man does not escape the notice of the gods (making him dear to them and turning apparent misfortunes into ultimate goods) (612e–613a), and eventually wins the race of life among men, securing honors, offices, and a glorious reputation, while the unjust are caught and punished (Book 10, 613c–e).
