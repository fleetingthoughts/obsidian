How does Alfred Ayer logically dismantle the claim of the transcendent metaphysician that one can have knowledge of a reality transcending the empirical world, starting from sensory premises?
#### Notes
**Epistemic Logic & Inferential Structure**: Deductive Refutation.<br><br>**Premise 1**: All cognitive reasoning and logical deductions must begin with premises derived from the evidence of our senses (empirical premises).<br><br>**Premise 2**: From empirical premises, no super-empirical properties, nor the existence of any super-empirical reality, can be logically inferred or validly deduced.<br><br>**Dialectical Counter-Argument**: The metaphysician asserts they are endowed with a unique faculty of non-sensory "intellectual intuition" that bypasses sensory premises, allowing them to directly apprehend a transcendent reality.<br><br>**Ayer's Refutation (Unstated Assumption)**: Criticizing the causal origin or psychological genesis of an assertion (e.g., appealing to intuition) does not logically prove its falsehood. Therefore, a successful critique must analyze the semantic nature of the statements themselves. Any statement referring to a reality transcending all possible sense-experience fails the semantic rules of language, rendering it a pseudo-proposition devoid of literal significance.<br><br>**Citation**: Chapter 1, Pages 33–34.
