What is the formal logical structure of an Inductive-Statistical (I-S) explanation, and how does it differ from a D-N explanation?
#### Notes
**Nature of Proposition: Conceptual Definition.** An I-S explanation subsumes a particular event under probabilistic laws rather than universal laws. Because the explanans does not deductively imply the explanandum, the argument is inductive: the premises make the conclusion highly probable. It is represented with a double line separating premises from conclusion, indicating an inductive logical relation: $$\begin{aligned} & \text{The probability of } O \text{ under conditions } R \text{ is high} \\ & \text{Case } i \text{ is a case of } R \\ \hline\hline & \text{Case } i \text{ is a case of } O \quad [r] \end{aligned}$$ (_Philosophy of Natural Science_, Chapter 5, pp. 58-59)
