To argue for the tripartite soul, Socrates lays down logical groundwork by defining relations between elements in a way that distinguishes between relations between elements and particulars of that element (438b-439d). For example, he examines our conventional use of language to distinguish between someone who's thirsty wanting a cold drink from someone who's thirsty wanting a hot drink, or knowledge itself and knowledge of particular area like medicine (438d-439a). What does his definition of relation mean for the two aforementioned examples and how is it used in conjunction with the principle of non-contradiction to distinguish the calculating part of the soul from the appetite.
