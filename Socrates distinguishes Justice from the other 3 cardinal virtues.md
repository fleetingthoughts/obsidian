In searching for the 4 cardinal virtues, Justice was identified by process of elimination, but what additional arguments does Socrates make to assert that his proposition of justice is separate from the other 3 cardinal virtues
#### Notes
Socrates argues that justice is distinct from the other 3 cardinal virtues through 2 criteria:
1) Whether it is required for a city to be well-governed
2) Whether its function and intent can be satisfied by the other 3 cardinal virtues. This has overlap with his definition of [dynamis in book 5](Socrates'%20definition%20of%20dynamis%20with%20respect%20to%20our%20cognitive%20faculties.md).
To this end he gives a few examples
- If a carpenter decides to be soldier or vice versa, that would result in the destruction of the city so justice satisfies criteria 1). Furthermore, wisdom, modesty, and courage does not address this particular scenario so it satisfies the 2nd criteria (434a–434c). I'm not entirely convinced of this latter argument by Socrates though.
- Socrates looks at a specific example with judges. The essence of law is to ensure that "no one have what belongs to others, nor be deprived of what belongs to him" (433-434a) to align his definition of justice with conventional modern practices. This references the definitions of justice he rebutted in book 1. Polemarchus and Cephalus had the right idea of what justice entails, but failed to distill the correct motivating principle or definition.
