What is the crucial difference between Immanuel Kant's rejection of transcendent metaphysics and Alfred Ayer's logical empiricist critique of metaphysics?
#### Notes
**Logical Analysis & Epistemological Distinction**: Contrast of factual limits versus logical limits of language.<br><br>**Kant's Position (Premise)**: Kant argued that the human understanding is constitutionally limited, such that when it attempts to think beyond possible experience (into the realm of _noumena_ or things-in-themselves), it inevitably falls into self-contradiction (_antinomies_). This makes the limit of knowledge a factual, psychological limit of human cognitive faculties.<br><br>**Ayer's Critique of Kant (Argument)**: To assert that something exists beyond the boundary of understanding, or to draw a factual boundary, one would have to succeed in passing that boundary to know both sides of it (preempting Wittgenstein’s principle: "in order to draw a limit to thinking, we should have to think both sides of this limit").<br><br>**Ayer's Alternative (Conclusion)**: Ayer establishes the boundary purely on the rules of _epistemic logic_ and the _significant use of language_. The metaphysician's failure is not that they attempt to employ their understanding in a field where it cannot factually go, but that they produce sentences that fail to conform to the semantic conditions under which alone a sentence can be literally significant.<br><br>**Citation**: Chapter 1, Page 34.
