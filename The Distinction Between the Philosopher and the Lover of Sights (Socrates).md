What logical premise does Socrates use to define a true lover or desirer of a subject and what is the philosopher a lover of and from whom is he distinguished from
#### Notes
**Premise.** Socrates establishes the definition (premise) that a true lover of any object (whether it be boys, wine, honor, or wisdom) does not merely love one part of it while rejecting another, but must cherish, desire, and embrace the _whole_ of that form (Book 5, 474c–475c).

The philosophers are defined as lovers of knowledge and the lovers of sight are excluded from this:
**• _Premise_: True philosophers are strictly defined as the lovers of the sight of truth (Book 5, 475e).<br>• _Argument_: Because the lovers of sights and sounds only delight in fair colors, shapes, and heard sounds (the particulars) but are cognitively unable to see, approach, or delight in the nature of the Beautiful itself (the Form), it is argued that they are merely "like" philosophers but do not possess true wisdom (Book 5, 475d–476b).
