How does Newtonian gravitation illustrate that a superior theory corrects, rather than simply deduces, previously established empirical laws like Kepler's and Galileo's laws?
#### Notes
**Nature of Proposition: Historical Case Example.** Newton's theory shows that Kepler's laws of planetary motion hold only approximately because planets exert gravitational pulls on each other, causing calculated orbital perturbations. Similarly, it shows Galileo's law of free fall ($s = 16t^2$) holds only approximately because the inverse-square law implies gravitational attraction (and thus acceleration) increases as the falling body gets closer to the center of the Earth, rather than remaining strictly constant. (_Philosophy of Natural Science_, Chapter 6, pp. 75-76)
