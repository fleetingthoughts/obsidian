How does Hempel define a "statement of universal form," and why is this a necessary but not a sufficient condition for being a law of nature?
#### Notes
**Nature of Proposition: Conceptual Definition.** A statement of universal form asserts a uniform connection between different empirical phenomena, stating that whenever and wherever conditions of kind $F$ occur, conditions of kind $G$ will always and without exception occur as well. This is not a sufficient condition because many true statements of universal form are merely "accidental generalizations" rather than genuine laws. (_Philosophy of Natural Science_, Chapter 5, p. 54)
