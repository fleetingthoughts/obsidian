What is the "Persuade or Obey" doctrine articulated by the Laws, and what room does it leave for citizen agency?
#### Notes
**Nature of Proposition**: Moral Duty / Dialectical Framework. **Philosophical Concept**: Political Obligation / Moral Disobedience. **Inferential Logic**: The Laws assert that a citizen has only two moral options: either "persuade" the state as to the true nature of justice, or "obey" its commands and endure whatever punishments (fines, imprisonment, blows, or death in war) it inflicts. If a citizen fails to persuade the state to change its laws or verdicts, they must submit. This establishes that the state's authority is absolute unless corrected through rational persuasion. **Citation**: _Crito_, 51b–c [Passage 8].
