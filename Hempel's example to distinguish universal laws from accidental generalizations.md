How does the contrast between the "gold" and "rocks" examples demonstrate the difference between a universal law and an accidental generalization?
#### Notes
**Nature of Proposition: Methodological Distinction.** The statement "all rocks in this box contain iron" is an accidental generalization because it simply happens to be the case. Similarly, "all bodies of pure gold have a mass of less than 100,000 kilograms" is an accidental generalization; even if true and well-confirmed, contemporary science does not preclude the possibility of producing a gold object exceeding that mass. In contrast, genuine laws represent deep, non-accidental physical uniformities. (_Philosophy of Natural Science_, Chapter 5, pp. 55-56)
