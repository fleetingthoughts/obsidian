Why does Hempel reject the assertion that an experimental test of a quantitative law (like the classical gas law) must keep all other environmental conditions strictly constant?
#### Notes
**Nature of Proposition: Methodological Argument.** Keeping absolutely all external conditions constant is physically impossible. Furthermore, a quantitative law (such as the gas law V = c * T/P) deductively implies that environmental factors not mentioned in the law (like humidity or magnetic field strength) are irrelevant to the phenomenon. Allowing these other factors to vary actually strengthens the test by exploring a wider range of cases for potential violations. (_Philosophy of Natural Science_, Chapter 3, pp. 20-21)
