What is the dual, unstable character of the Oligarchic man, and why does he lack true virtue?
#### Notes
**Argued Position.**<br>• _Premise_: True virtue requires a single-minded, harmonized soul that does right through rational persuasion rather than fear.<br>• _Argument_: Because the oligarchic man has not devoted himself to education, dronelike desires of the beggar and wrongdoing variety exist within him. Although he has a good reputation in contracts, he is merely holding down these bad desires by necessity and fear because he trembles for his property. It is argued that his soul is divided by an internal faction where his better desires master his worse ones only through force; thus, he is twofold rather than one, and the true virtue of a harmonized soul escapes him (Book 8, 554b–e).
