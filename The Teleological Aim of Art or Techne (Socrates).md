When Thrasymachus defines the "precise ruler" as one who never errs and acts only for his own advantage, how does Socrates use the _directional aim of an art (techne)_ to refute him?
#### Notes
Socrates demonstrates that every art in its precise sense (like medicine or piloting) serves and perfects the subject it operates upon, not the practitioner (341c–342c). Therefore, a precise ruler commands strictly for the advantage of the ruled (the weaker), not himself (342e)
