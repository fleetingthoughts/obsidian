How does Karl Popper define the relation between simplicity, empirical content, and falsifiability, and how does Hempel critique this definition?
#### Notes
**Nature of Proposition: Epistemological Argument.** Popper argues that simpler hypotheses tell us more (have greater empirical content) and are more severely testable or falsifiable. Hempel critiques this by showing that conjoining two unrelated laws (e.g., Hooke's and Snell's laws) increases empirical content but decreases simplicity. Furthermore, competing polynomial hypotheses of different degrees of simplicity do not differ in falsifiability, as any one of them can be falsified with the exact same ease by a single counter-instance. (_Philosophy of Natural Science_, Chapter 4, pp. 43-44)
