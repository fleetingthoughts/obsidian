How does the acceptability of a universal law depend on contemporary scientific theories?
#### Notes
**Nature of Proposition: Epistemological Premise.** A statement of universal form, even if empirically well-confirmed and presumably true, will not qualify as a law if it rules out hypothetical occurrences that accepted theories qualify as physically possible (such as fusing two gold bodies to exceed 100,000 kg). Conversely, an untested or unconfirmed statement will qualify as a theoretical law if it is logically implied by an accepted theory. (_Philosophy of Natural Science_, Chapter 5, p. 58
