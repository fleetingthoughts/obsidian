How does Socrates resolve Thrasymachus's shepherd objection using "precise speech" and the separation of crafts?
#### Notes
He argues that the shepherd's art, in precise speech, is solely concerned with providing the best for the sheep over which it is set (Book 1, 345c–d). Fattening sheep for slaughter belongs to the art of feasting, and selling them belongs to the money-making art (Book 1, 345d). Thus, the primary craft itself remains dedicated strictly to the benefit of its subject, independent of any external profit motives (Book 1, 345d–e).
