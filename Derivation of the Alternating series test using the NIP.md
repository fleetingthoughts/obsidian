Derive the alternating series test using the Nested Interval Property
#### Notes
**Setup:** Let $(a_n)$ be a sequence that is decreasing ($a_1 \geq a_2 \geq a_3 \geq \dots$) and converges to zero ($\lim_{n \to \infty} a_n = 0$). We want to show that the alternating series $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty} (-1)^{n+1}a_n$ converges. Let $s_n$ represent the $n$-th partial sum of this series: $$ s_n = a_1 - a_2 + a_3 - \dots \pm a_n $$ *** ### Proof 1. **Analyze the subsequences of partial sums:** Look at the even partial sums: $$ s_{2k} = s_{2k-2} + a_{2k-1} - a_{2k} $$ Since the sequence $(a_n)$ is decreasing, $a_{2k-1} - a_{2k} \geq 0$, which means $s_{2k} \geq s_{2k-2}$. Therefore, the sequence of **even partial sums is increasing**. Now look at the odd partial sums: $$ s_{2k+1} = s_{2k-1} - a_{2k} + a_{2k+1} $$ Since $a_{2k} \geq a_{2k+1}$, the term $-(a_{2k} - a_{2k+1}) \leq 0$, meaning $s_{2k+1} \leq s_{2k-1}$. Therefore, the sequence of **odd partial sums is decreasing**. 2. **Establish the bounds:** For any $k$, the difference between consecutive partial sums is: $$ s_{2k-1} - s_{2k} = a_{2k} $$ Because all $a_n \geq 0$, we know that $s_{2k} \leq s_{2k-1}$ for all $k$. Furthermore, every even partial sum is bounded above by $s_1$, and every odd partial sum is bounded below by $s_2$. 3. **Construct the nested intervals:** Define a sequence of closed intervals $I_k = [s_{2k}, s_{2k-1}]$. Because the lower bounds (even sums) are increasing and the upper bounds (odd sums) are decreasing, these intervals are nested: $$ I_1 \supseteq I_2 \supseteq I_3 \supseteq \dots $$ 4. **Apply the Nested Interval Property (NIP):** By the NIP, the intersection of these nested closed intervals is non-empty. Thus, there exists at least one real number $S$ such that $S \in I_k$ for all $k$. 5. **Show uniqueness and convergence:** The length of the $k$-th interval is $s_{2k-1} - s_{2k} = a_{2k}$. Since we are given that $\lim_{n \to \infty} a_n = 0$, the length of the intervals converges to $0$. This implies that the intersection $\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty} I_k$ contains exactly one point, $S$. Because the partial sums $s_n$ are bounded within these intervals whose lengths shrink to zero, it follows that: $$ \lim_{n \to \infty} s_n = S $$ Therefore, the alternating series converges.
