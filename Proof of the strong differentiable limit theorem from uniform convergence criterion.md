Prove: Let $(f_n)$ be a sequence of differentiable functions on $[a, b]$ such that $(f_n')$ converges uniformly to $g$ on $[a, b]$ and $(f_n(x_0))$ converges for some $x_0 \in [a, b]$. Assuming the differentiable limit theorem and the single-point uniform convergence criterion, show that $(f_n)$ converges uniformly to a differentiable function $f$ with $f' = g$.
#### Notes
By the single-point uniform convergence criterion, uniform convergence of $(f_n')$ and convergence of $(f_n(x_0))$ imply that $(f_n)$ converges uniformly (hence pointwise) to a limit $f$ on $[a, b]$. By the [[Understanding Analysis - 6.3 Uniform Convergence and Differentiation]]differentiable limit theorem, $f$ is differentiable on $[a, b]$ and $f' = g$.
