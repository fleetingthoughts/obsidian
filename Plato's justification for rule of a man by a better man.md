How does Socrates justify the rule of the best man over the slave or the child, and what is the ultimate goal of the law?
#### Notes
**Argued Position.**<br>• _Premise_: It is better for all human beings to be ruled by what is divine and prudent (Book 9, 590d).<br>• _Argument_: To ensure that everyone is as alike and friendly as possible, those who cannot rule the beasts within themselves must be the "slaves" of the best man who has the divine rule within himself. This is not for the slave's detriment, but to ensure they are piloted by the same divine principle (590d). Similarly, the law and the rule over children maintain control and do not set them free until we have established an internal guardian and ruler in their souls to take our place (Book 9, 590d–591a).
