How does Carl Hempel translate the traditional, vague metaphysical dispute between mechanism and vitalism into a clear, testable epistemological thesis?
#### Notes
**Nature of Proposition: Conceptual Definition / Reformulation.** Hempel reformulates the mechanism-vitalism dispute into the thesis of the "reducibility of biology to physics and chemistry." This thesis is divided into two distinct logical claims: first, that all biological concepts can be defined in terms of the concepts of physics and chemistry ($M'_1$), and second, that all biological laws can be deductively derived from the laws and theoretical principles of physics and chemistry ($M'_2$). (_Philosophy of Natural Science_, Chapter 8, pp. 101–102)
