After distinguishing necessary and unnecessary desires in Book 8, how does Socrates expand on the definition of unnecessary desires and when they typically manifest
#### Notes
**Premise.** Socrates establishes that among the unnecessary desires, there is a class of savage, lawless desires that are hostile to law and exist in everyone. While they are usually checked in the waking state by laws, reason, and better desires, they manifest in dreams during sleep when the calculating, tame, and ruling part of the soul slumbers, leaving the beastly, wild part to seek the uninhibited satisfaction of its base inclinations (e.g., incest, murder, and total shamelessness) (Book 9, 571a–572b).
