Socrates's evaluation of how wealth and poverty structurally affect both the craftsmen and the integrity of the city.
#### Notes
The soldiers must protect the city against wealth and luxury for they taint virtue (421d–422a). To do so, they must also limit the size of the city.

The protection of education requires special attention because its corruption can manifest in the most innocuous ways. In line with the impressionability of the youth in Book 2-3, the corruption of a children's education can even manifest in the kinds of play they engage with.

The guardians must protect their educational values (423e–424d). Socrates highlights the value of education by citing Damon's thesis on arts and political order; and the necessity of education in regulating transactions where the letter of the law cannot hope to manage:
- He compares market contracts to the head of a hydra (425c–427a).
- Commonplace manners and customs required for a ordered city are dictated by education where laws cannot hope to regulate
