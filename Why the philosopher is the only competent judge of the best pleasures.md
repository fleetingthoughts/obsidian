Why is the philosopher (the lover of wisdom) argued to be the only competent and sovereign judge of which of the three lives is the sweetest?
#### Notes
**Argued Position.**<br>• _Premise_: A fine judgment of pleasures must be made using three criteria: **experience, prudence, and argument** (Book 9, 582a).<br>• _Argument_: It is argued that: (1) only the philosopher has **experience** of all three pleasures, having tasted the pleasure of gain from childhood and the pleasure of honor, while the money-maker has never tasted the sweet pleasure of knowing truth (582b–c); (2) only the philosopher possesses **prudence**, which is necessary for a correct evaluation (582d); and (3) only the philosopher possesses **argument**, which is the specific dialectical instrument of judgment (582d). Therefore, the philosopher's praise of the wisdom-loving life is the absolute truth (Book 9, 582d–583a).
