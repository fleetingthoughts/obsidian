Why do interpretative sentences that provide criteria for overlapping operational methods (e.g., mercury and alcohol thermometers) combine stipulative and descriptive functions?
#### Notes
**Nature of Proposition: Logical Argument.** A standard stipulative definition merely introduces a term without adding factual information. However, when a scientific term has overlapping operational criteria, the statement that these different procedures yield the same numerical result (e.g., mercury and alcohol thermometers give equal readings where they overlap) functions as an empirical generalization that can be true or false; thus, these interpretative sentences combine stipulative defining functions with descriptive, empirical claims. (_Philosophy of Natural Science_, Chapter 7, p. 98)
