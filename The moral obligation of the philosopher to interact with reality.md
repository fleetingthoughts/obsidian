Why is the state justified in compelling the philosophers to go back down into the cave to rule, even if it seems to make their lives worse?
#### Notes
**Argued Position.**<br>• _Premise_: The ultimate purpose of the law is not to secure the happiness of any single class, but to harmonize and bind the entire city together by persuasion and compulsion (Book 7, 519e–520a).<br>• _Argument_: Because the city's founders actively bred and educated the philosophers to be leaders and kings, and because they owe their superior education to the city's laws (unlike philosophers in other cities who grow up spontaneously), it is argued that it is entirely just to compel them to take their turn in ruling and caring for the other citizens (Book 7, 520a–c).
