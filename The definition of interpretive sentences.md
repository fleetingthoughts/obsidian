What are "interpretative sentences" in a scientific theory, and why are they necessary for theoretical terms?
#### Notes
**Nature of Proposition: Conceptual Definition.** Interpretative sentences are statements that specify the meanings of theoretical terms proper (the characteristic terms of a theory) by means of an antecedently available, pretheoretical vocabulary. They are necessary because theoretical terms (which refer to unobservables) have no direct empirical content, and we require interpretative sentences to link them to terms we already understand, thereby instilling empirical content and making the theory testable. (_Philosophy of Natural Science_, Chapter 7, pp. 87–88).

I feel these are sentences that have factual content and are not tautological unlike the descriptive and stipulative definitions also defined in Chapter 7. In other words, interpretive sentences are a kind of synthetic statement
