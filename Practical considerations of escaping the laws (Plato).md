What dialectical arguments do the Laws use to refute Crito's claim that escaping is necessary to protect Socrates' friends and children?
#### Notes
**Nature of Proposition**: Dialectical Counter-Argument / Pragmatic Refutation. **Philosophical Concept**: Consequentialist Ethics / Ethical Consistency. **Inferential Logic**: Regarding friends: escaping will put Socrates' friends in danger of exile, disfranchisement, and loss of property (proving Crito's plan harms the very people he wishes to help). Regarding children: if Socrates takes his children to Thessaly to raise them, he makes them "strangers" and deprives them of Athenian citizenship. If he leaves them behind, his friends will raise them anyway, which they can do just as well if Socrates is in the underworld as when he is alive in exile. **Citation**: _Crito_, 53a–b, 54a [Passage 9, 10].
