How does the "painter of regimes" metaphor outline the method by which a philosopher-king would establish a just state?
#### Notes
**Argued Position.**<br>• _Premise_: A philosopher-king cannot build a just city upon a corrupt foundation (501a).<br>• _Argument_: It is argued that the philosopher must treat the city and human characters as a blank tablet: first, they must wipe the tablet clean (by separating children from their corrupt parents/customs) (501a); next, they must outline the shape of the regime, constantly looking back and forth between the natural Forms (the Just, Fair, and Moderate by nature) and the human characters they are shaping, blending practices to paint the "image of man" to be as dear to the gods as possible (Book 6, 501a–502a).
