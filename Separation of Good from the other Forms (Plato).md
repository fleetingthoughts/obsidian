How does Socrates argue that the Idea of the Good transcends even Being (_ousia_) itself?
#### Notes
**Argued Position.**<br>• _Premise_: The sun not only provides objects of sight with the power to be seen, but also provides them with generation, growth, and nourishment, though the sun itself is not generation (509b).<br>• _Argument_: By analogical scaling, it is argued that the Idea of the Good not only provides the objects of knowledge (the Forms) with their capacity to be known, but also provides them with their very existence and being (_ousia_). Since the cause of being must transcend its effects, the Good is argued to be not Being itself, but something "beyond being," exceeding it in dignity and power (Book 6, 509b–c).
