How does the physical mechanism of drinking from the River of Lethe in the Myth of Er illustrate the salvific role of prudence (_phronesis_)? (Book 10, 621a–c)
#### Notes
**Argued Position.**<br>• _Premise_: All souls must march through the stifling heat of the plain of Lethe and camp by the river of Carelessness, whose water no vessel can contain (621a).<br>• _Argument_: While it is a necessity for all souls to drink a certain measure of the water before being reincarnated, it is argued that **those who are not saved by prudence drink more than the measure**, immediately forgetting everything they learned in the afterlife (621a–b). The wise, possessing the internal restraint of prudence, drink only the necessary minimum, keeping their souls unpolluted and preserving their cognitive affinity for truth across birth (Book 10, 621a–c).
