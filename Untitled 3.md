How does Ayer refute the objection that defining philosophy strictly as logical analysis is an arbitrary departure from historical philosophical tradition, using John Locke as an example?
#### Notes
**Nature of Propositions**: Historiographical Reinterpretation & Definition of Philosophy.<br><br>**Dialectical Counter-Argument**: Critics claim that historical philosophy consists almost entirely of speculative metaphysical system-building.<br>**Ayer's Counter-Thesis**: The major traditional philosophers were primarily engaged in logical analysis rather than metaphysics.<br>**Analysis of Locke's Epistemology**:<br>1. _Premise_: Locke explicitly defined his task not as building speculative systems, but as an "under-labourer" clearing the ground for knowledge.<br>2. _Argument_: Locke did not attempt an _a priori_ justification of common-sense beliefs; instead, he performed analytical work—classifying propositions, defining the logical limits of human understanding, and analyzing the concept of material substance.<br>**Conclusion**: The non-metaphysical, enduring core of Locke's work is an exemplar of philosophical analysis. Any non-analytical portions of his text belong properly to empirical psychology, not metaphysics.<br>**Citation**: Chapter 2, Pages 51–52.
