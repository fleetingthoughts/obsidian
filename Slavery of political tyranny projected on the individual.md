How does Socrates compare the slavery of a tyrannical city to the slavery of the individual?
#### Notes
**Argued Position.**<br>• _Premise_: The individual soul corresponds structurally to the political state: as a city is to a city in virtue and happiness, so is a man to a man (Book 9, 576c–577c).<br>• _Argument_: Because a city under a tyranny is enslaved in the highest degree—with its most decent citizens disenfranchised and a small, depraved ruling elite holding power—it is argued that the tyrannic soul must also be filled with extreme slavery and illiberality. The best, most decent parts of his mind are enslaved, while a small, maddened, and depraved part acts as master (Book 9, 577c–d).
