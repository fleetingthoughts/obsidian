Name the 3 classifications of "what is good", that is made in the beginning of book 2 of the republic and where both Socrates and Glaucon classifies Justice
#### Notes
Glaucon divides all "goods" into three distinct categories:
1. **Goods desired for their own sake only:** Things we choose purely for enjoyment and immediate pleasure, without regard to future consequences (e.g., harmless pleasures) (357b).
2. **Goods desired for their own sake AND for their consequences:** Things we value both in themselves and for what comes out of them (e.g., thinking, seeing, being healthy) (357c).
3. **Goods desired ONLY for their consequences / wages:** Things burdensome or drudgery in themselves, but pursued for money, reputation, or beneficial outcomes (e.g., physical training, medical treatment, practicing a trade) (357c–d).

**Arguments (358a)**
- **Socrates’ Position:** Socrates argues that justice belongs in the **second (finest) class**—worth having both for its own sake and for what comes out of it (358a).
- **Popular / Mass Opinion:** Glaucon points out that the majority places justice in the **third class**—a form of drudgery practiced for wages and reputation, but fled from as something inherently hard (358a).
