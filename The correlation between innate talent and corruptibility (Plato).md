Why does Socrates assert that the finest philosophic natures are most prone to becoming exceptionally corrupt?
#### Notes
**Argued Position.**<br>• _Premise_: Every seed or growing thing, the more vigorous it is, the more severely it degenerates when deprived of its appropriate nourishment, climate, or soil, since bad is more opposed to the good than to the ordinary (491b–d).<br>• _Argument_: Because the philosophic nature is the most vigorous and refined of all human souls, it is argued that when it is subjected to an inappropriate rearing and bad instruction, it suffers a far worse degeneration than ordinary, weaker natures, which are incapable of either great good or great evil (Book 6, 491d–492a).
