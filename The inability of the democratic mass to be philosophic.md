On what logical grounds does Socrates deduce that a democratic populace can never be philosophic?
#### Notes
**Argued Position.**<br>• _Premise_: A multitude is cognitively incapable of accepting or believing that the "Form itself" (such as Beauty itself or Justice itself) exists as a unified reality rather than the many shifting particulars (493e–494a).<br>• _Argument_: Because philosophy requires the cognitive grasp of the unchanging Forms, and because the mob can only perceive the diverse, sensible particulars, it is argued that a multitude can never be philosophic, making it inevitable that they will blame and persecute those who do philosophize (Book 6, 494a).
